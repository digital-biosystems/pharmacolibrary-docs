# Hydrochloric acid

- **generic name:** Hydrochloric acid
- **T3DB:** [T3D4968](http://www.t3db.ca/toxins/T3D4968) · **PubChem:** [CID 313](https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/compound/313)
- **groups:** investigational

## About

HCl refers to both hydrogen chloride gas and aqueous hydrochloric acid. Hydrogen chloride gas is a colorless gas formed from the reaction of hydrogen with chlorine. It forms white fumes of hydrochloric acid upon contact with atmospheric humidity. …

- **Category:** Natural Toxin, Industrial/Workplace Toxin
- **Route of exposure:** inhalation, ingestion, dermal, eyes
- **Sources and uses:** Hydrochloric acid is found naturally in gastric acid. Most hydrogen chloride gas is used in the production of hydrochloric acid. …
- **Mechanism of toxicity:** Both hydrogen chloride gas and hydrochloric acid are highly corrosive. Many strong acids cause tissue burns through the denaturation of proteins and partial hydrolysis of proteins. …
- **Health effects:** Hydrogen chloride forms corrosive hydrochloric acid on contact with water found in body tissue. Inhalation of the fumes can cause coughing, choking, inflammation of the nose, throat, and upper respiratory tract, and in severe cases, pulmonary edema, circulatory system failure, and death. …
- **Lethal dose:** 2857 ug/kg (Oral), 1300 ppm/30 minutes (inhalation)
- **Carcinogenicity:** IARC group 3, not classifiable as to its carcinogenicity to humans.
- **Minimal risk levels (ATSDR):** Occupational exposure limits for hydrogen chloride (gas) have been set at 5 ppm
- **Acute toxicity:** LD50 [oral, rat]; 700 mg/kg; LD50 [rat]; 3124 ppm (1 hour)
- **Treatment:** The mainstay of treatment of any acid burn is copious irrigation with large amounts of tap water. …
