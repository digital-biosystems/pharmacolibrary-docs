# Ethanol

- **generic name:** Ethanol
- **T3DB:** [T3D0770](http://www.t3db.ca/toxins/T3D0770) · **DrugBank:** [DB00898](https://go.drugbank.com/drugs/DB00898) · **PubChem:** [CID 702](https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/compound/702)
- **groups:** approved, investigational

## About

Ethanol (also called ethyl alcohol, grain alcohol, drinking alcohol, or simply alcohol) is an organic compound with the chemical formula CH3CH2OH. It is an alcohol, with its formula also written as C2H5OH, C2H6O, or EtOH, where Et is the pseudoelement symbol for ethyl. Ethanol is a volatile, flammable, colorless liquid with a pungent taste. As a central nervous system (CNS) depressant, it is the active ingredient in alcoholic beverages, and the second most consumed drug globally behind caffeine.

<p><small>Source: <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethanol?oldid=1385751364">Wikipedia</a> · <a href="https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/">CC BY-SA 4.0</a></small></p>

- **Category:** Drug, Household Toxin, Industrial/Workplace Toxin, Food Toxin, Natural Toxin
- **Route of exposure:** oral, rapidly absorbed. Blood alcohol level and the time necessary to achieve it are controlled largely by the rapidity and extent of ethanol consumption.
- **Sources and uses:** Ethanol is used as a solvent in industry, in many household products and pharmaceuticals, and in intoxicating beverages. It is also used for therapeutic neurolysis of nerves or ganglia for the relief of intractable chronic pain in such conditions as inoperable cancer and trigeminal neuralgia (tic douloureux), in patients for whom neurosurgical procedures are contraindicated.
- **Mechanism of toxicity:** Alcohol binds to the GABA(A) receptors (delta subunit), NMDA receptors, Glycine receptors, Serotonin receptors, Acetylcholine receptors, L-channel calcium channels and GIRK channels. Ethanol acts in the central nervous system primarily by binding to the GABAA receptor, increasing the effects of the inhibitory neurotransmitter GABA. …
- **Health effects:** Acute: At 0.1% blood alcohol levels individuals experience CNS depression, nausea, possible vomiting, impaired cognition and impaired motor and sensory function. Accidents or injury can also occur due to the side effects of loss of coordination, slowed reaction time, sleepiness and impaired judgment. …
- **Lethal dose:** The fatal dose of ethanol is 300-400 mL of pure ethanol (600-800 mL of 50% spirits), for the average adult if consumed in less than one hour. …
- **Carcinogenicity:** Ethanol in alcoholic beverages is carcinogenic to humans (Group 1).
- **Acute toxicity:** LD50: 5628 mg/kg (Oral, rat)
- **Treatment:** If you suspect someone has alcohol poisoning, call for an ambulance to take them to a hospital. …
- **Detected in:** blood, cerebrospinal fluid, feces, saliva, urine
