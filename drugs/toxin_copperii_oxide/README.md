# Copper(II) oxide

- **generic name:** Copper(II) oxide

## About

Copper(II) oxide is an oxide of copper. It occurs naturally as the mineral tenorite. Copper(II) oxide is used as a pigment in ceramics, in the production of rayon, as a dietary supplement, in dry and wet cell batteries, for welding copper alloys, and as a semiconductor. …

- **Category:** Household Toxin, Industrial/Workplace Toxin, Natural Toxin
- **Route of exposure:** oral, inhalation, dermal
- **Sources and uses:** Copper(II) oxide is used as a pigment in ceramics, in the production of rayon, as a dietary supplement, in dry and wet cell batteries, for welding copper alloys, and as a semiconductor.
- **Mechanism of toxicity:** Excess copper is sequestered within hepatocyte lysosomes, where it is complexed with metallothionein. Copper hepatotoxicity is believed to occur when the lysosomes become saturated and copper accumulates in the nucleus, causing nuclear damage. …
- **Health effects:** People must absorb small amounts of copper every day because copper is essential for good health, however, high levels of copper can be harmful. Very-high doses of copper can cause damage to your liver and kidneys, and can even cause death. …
- **Lethal dose:** 10 to 20 grams for an adult human (copper salts).
- **Minimal risk levels (ATSDR):** Acute Oral: 0.01 mg/kg/day 
Intermediate Oral: 0.01 mg/kg/day
- **Acute toxicity:** LD50: 470 mg/kg (Oral, Rat); LD50: 273 mg/kg (Intraperitoneal, Mouse)
- **Treatment:** EYES: irrigate opened eyes for several minutes under running water. …
- **Detected in:** blood, cerebrospinal fluid, saliva, urine
