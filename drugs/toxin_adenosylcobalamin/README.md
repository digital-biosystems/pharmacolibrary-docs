# Adenosylcobalamin

- **generic name:** Adenosylcobalamin
- **T3DB:** [T3D0672](http://www.t3db.ca/toxins/T3D0672) · **PubChem:** [CID 6436143](https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/compound/6436143)
- **groups:** approved, nutraceutical

## About

Adenosylcobalamin is one of two metabolically active forms synthesized upon ingestion of vitamin B12 and is the predominant form in the liver; it acts as a coenzyme in the reaction catalyzed by methylmalonyl-CoA mutase. A cobalamin (cbl) derivative in which the substituent is deoxyadenosyl. It is one of two metabolically active forms synthesized upon ingestion of vitamin B12 and is the predominant form in the liver; it acts as a coenzyme in the reaction catalyzed by methylmalonyl-CoA mutase (MCM; E.C. 5.4.99.2). …

- **Category:** Pollutant, Airborne Pollutant, Food Toxin, Natural Toxin
- **Route of exposure:** inhalation, oral, dermal
- **Sources and uses:** Cobamamide is a form of vitamin B12.
- **Mechanism of toxicity:** Cobalt is believed to exhibit its toxicity through a oxidant-based and free radical-based processes. It produces oxygen radicals and may be oxidized to ionic cobalt, causing increased lipid peroxidation, DNA damage, and inducing certain enzymes that lead to cell apoptosis. …
- **Health effects:** Exposure to high amount of cobalt can cause heart, lung, kidney, and liver damage. Skin contact is known to result in contact dermatitis. …
- **Carcinogenicity:** IARC group 2B, possibly carcinogenic to humans.
- **Minimal risk levels (ATSDR):** Chronic Inhalation: 0.0001 mg/m3; Intermediate Oral: 0.01 mg/kg/day
- **Acute toxicity:** LD50: 5000 mg/kg (Oral, Guinea pig)
- **Treatment:** Treatment of cobalt poisoning is symptomatic.
