# Ergine

- **generic name:** Ergine
- **T3DB:** [T3D3684](http://www.t3db.ca/toxins/T3D3684) · **PubChem:** [CID 442072](https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/compound/442072)

## About

Ergine is an alkaloid of the ergoline family. Like other ergoline alkaloids, it occurs in various species of vines of the Convolvulaceae (morning glory) family and in some species of lower fungi. As the dominant alkaloid in the hallucinogenic seeds of Rivea corymbosa, Argyreia nervosa (Hawaiian baby woodrose) and Ipomoea tricolor (morning glory), it is often stated that ergine and/or isoergine (its epimer) is responsible for the psychedelic activity, though this has not been proven. …

- **Category:** Fungal Toxin, Natural Toxin
- **Route of exposure:** oral, dermal, inhalation, and parenteral (contaminated drugs).
- **Sources and uses:** Ergoline alkaloids occur in various species of vines of the Convolvulaceae (morning glory) family and in some species of lower fungi. Ergine is the dominant alkaloid in the hallucinogenic seeds of Rivea corymbosa, Argyreia nervosa (Hawaiian baby woodrose) and Ipomoea tricolor (morning glory), and may be responsible for their psychedelic activity, though this has not been proven.
- **Mechanism of toxicity:** Ergoline alkaloids tend to act as a group, producing complex and variable effects of partial agonism or antagonism at adrenergic, dopaminergic, and serotonergic receptors. Variables relating to these effects are influenced by the agent, dosage, species, tissue, physiological, and endocrinological state, and experimental conditions. …
- **Health effects:** Ingestion of ergoline alkaloids is known to cause the disease ergotism. Ergotism occurs in two forms, gangrenous and convulsive, likely depending on the different kinds and amounts of ergoline alkaloids present.
- **Treatment:** Treatment for ergotism consists of vasodilators, anticoagulants and low molecular weight dextrans. …
