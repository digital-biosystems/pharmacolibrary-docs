# Phenytoin

- **generic name:** Phenytoin
- **T3DB:** [T3D2728](http://www.t3db.ca/toxins/T3D2728) · **DrugBank:** [DB00252](https://go.drugbank.com/drugs/DB00252) · **PubChem:** [CID 1775](https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/compound/1775)
- **groups:** approved, investigational, vet_approved

## About

An anticonvulsant that is used in a wide variety of seizures. It is also an anti-arrhythmic and a muscle relaxant. The mechanism of therapeutic action is not clear, although several cellular actions have been described including effects on ion channels, active transport, and general membrane stabilization. …

- **Category:** Drug, Synthetic Toxin
- **Route of exposure:** intravenous, Oral, Intramuscular.
Bioavailability 70-100% oral, 24.4% for rectal and intravenous administration. Rapid rate of absorption with peak blood concentration expected in 1&frac12, to 3 hours.
- **Sources and uses:** For the control of generalized tonic-clonic (grand mal) and complex partial (psychomotor, temporal lobe) seizures and prevention and treatment of seizures occurring during or following neurosurgery.
- **Mechanism of toxicity:** Phenytoin acts on sodium channels on the neuronal cell membrane, limiting the spread of seizure activity and reducing seizure propagation. By promoting sodium efflux from neurons, phenytoin tends to stabilize the threshold against hyperexcitability caused by excessive stimulation or environmental changes capable of reducing membrane sodium gradient. …
- **Health effects:** May cause a potentially dangerous rash that may develop into Stevens Johnson syndrome, an extremely rare but potentially fatal skin disease.
- **Carcinogenicity:** IARC group 2B, possibly carcinogenic to humans.
- **Acute toxicity:** Oral, mouse: LD<sub>50</sub> = 150 mg/kg; Oral, rat: LD<sub>50</sub> = 1635 mg/kg.
