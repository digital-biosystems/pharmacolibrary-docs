# Cobalt-60

- **generic name:** Cobalt-60
- **T3DB:** [T3D4973](http://www.t3db.ca/toxins/T3D4973) · **PubChem:** [CID 61492](https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/compound/61492)

## About

Cobalt-60, <sup>60</sup>Co, is a synthetic radioactive isotope of cobalt with a half-life of 5.2714 years. It is produced artificially by neutron activation of the isotope <sup>59</sup>Co. <sup>60</sup>Co decays by beta decay to the stable isotope nickel-60 (<sup>60</sup>Ni). Cobalt is a metallic element with the atomic number 27. …

- **Category:** Industrial/Workplace Toxin, Synthetic Toxin
- **Route of exposure:** inhalational, oral, dermal, radiation
- **Sources and uses:** Radioactive cobalt is used for commercial and medical purposes, such as for sterilizing medical equipment and consumer products, radiation therapy for treating cancer patients, manufacturing plastics, and irradiating food.
- **Mechanism of toxicity:** Cobalt is believed to exhibit its toxicity through a oxidant-based and free radical-based processes. It produces oxygen radicals and may be oxidized to ionic cobalt, causing increased lipid peroxidation, DNA damage, and inducing certain enzymes that lead to cell apoptosis. …
- **Health effects:** Exposure to high amount of cobalt can cause heart, lung, kidney, and liver damage. Skin contact is known to result in contact dermatitus. …
- **Carcinogenicity:** Internalized radionuclides that emit β particles are carcinogenic to humans (Group 1). …
- **Minimal risk levels (ATSDR):** Chronic Inhalation: 0.0001 mg/m3
Intermediate Oral: 0.01 mg/kg/day
Acute Radiation: 4 mSv 
Chronic Radiation: 1 mSv/yr
- **Acute toxicity:** LD50: 503 mg/kg (Oral, Rat) LD50: 25 mg/kg (Intravenous, Rabbit)
- **Treatment:** Treatment of cobalt poisoning is symptomatic.
- **Detected in:** blood, cerebrospinal fluid, saliva, urine
