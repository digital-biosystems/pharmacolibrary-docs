## About

Aluminium is a soft, lightweight metal with appearance ranging from silvery to dull gray, depending on the surface roughness. Data indicate that aluminum contaminates much of the raw material used to manufacture solutions used for intravenous nutritional support of hospitalized and ambulatory patients, and that pharmaceutical manufacturers have only recently obtained the technology necessary to detect aluminum contamination of their products. As a result, aluminum bypassed normal barriers and entered the blood, accumulating in tissues such as bone, liver and the central nervous system with toxic consequences. …

- **Category:** Drug, Household Toxin, Industrial/Workplace Toxin, Food Toxin, Natural Toxin
- **Route of exposure:** oral, inhalation
- **Sources and uses:** Aluminum is used for beverage cans, pots and pans, airplanes, siding and roofing, and foil. It is often mixed with small amounts of other metals to form aluminum alloys, which are stronger and harder. …
- **Mechanism of toxicity:** The main targets of aluminum are the central nervous system and bones. Aluminum binds to dietary phosphorus and impairs gastrointestinal absorption of phosphorus. …
- **Health effects:** Aluminum targets the nervous system and causes decreased nervous system performance and is associated with altered function of the blood-brain barrier. The accumulation of aluminum in the body may cause bone or brain diseases. …
- **Carcinogenicity:** Not listed by IARC. …
- **Minimal risk levels (ATSDR):** Intermediate Oral: 1.0 mg/kg/day
Chronic Oral: 1.0 mg/kg/day
- **Detected in:** blood, cerebrospinal fluid, saliva, urine
