# Saxitoxin

- **generic name:** Saxitoxin
- **T3DB:** [T3D2551](http://www.t3db.ca/toxins/T3D2551) · **PubChem:** [CID 37165](https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/compound/37165)

## About

Saxitoxin is a paralytic poison from Alaska butter clams (<i>Saxidomus giganteus</i>), toxic mussels (<i>Mytilus californianus</i>), the plankton <i>Gonyaulax cantenella</i> and <i>Protogonyaulax tamarensis</i>. Ingestion of saxitoxin (usually through shellfish contaminated by toxic algal blooms) is responsible for the human illness known as paralytic shellfish poisoning. Saxitoxin (STX) is a neurotoxin naturally produced by certain species of marine dinoflagellates (<i>Alexandrium sp.</i>, <i>Gymnodinium sp.</i>, <i>Pyrodinium sp.</i>) and cyanobacteria (<i>Anabaena sp.</i>, some <i>Aphanizomenon</i> species, <i>Cylindrospermopsis sp.</i>, <i>Lyngbya sp.</i>, <i>Planktothrix sp.</i>). (Wikipedia) Saxitoxin has been shown to exhibit emetic function.

- **Category:** Animal Toxin, Bacterial Toxin, Food Toxin, Natural Toxin
- **Route of exposure:** injection (sting/bite)
- **Sources and uses:** Saxitoxin is naturally produced by certain species of marine dinoflagellates (genus Alexandrium, Gymnodinium, Pyrodinium) and cyanobacteria (genus Anabaena, Aphanizomenon, Cylindrospermopsis, Lyngbya, Planktothrix). It is found in puffer fish and shellfish and is responsible for the human illness known as paralytic shellfish poisoning (PSP).
- **Mechanism of toxicity:** Saxitoxin blocks voltage-gated sodium channels of nerve cells.
- **Health effects:** Saxitoxin is a neurotoxin. It causes a syndrome known as paralytic shellfish poisoning, which produces paralysis and may be fatal due to respiratory failure in extreme cases.
