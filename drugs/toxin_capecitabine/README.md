# Capecitabine

- **generic name:** Capecitabine
- **T3DB:** [T3D3507](http://www.t3db.ca/toxins/T3D3507) · **DrugBank:** [DB01101](https://go.drugbank.com/drugs/DB01101) · **PubChem:** [CID 60953](https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/compound/60953)
- **groups:** approved, investigational

## About

Capecitabine is an orally-administered chemotherapeutic agent used in the treatment of metastatic breast and colorectal cancers. Capecitabine is a prodrug, that is enzymatically converted to fluorouracil (antimetabolite) in the tumor, where it inhibits DNA synthesis and slows growth of tumor tissue.

- **Category:** Drug, Synthetic Toxin
- **Route of exposure:** readily absorbed through the GI tract (~70%)
- **Sources and uses:** For the treatment of patients with metastatic breast cancer resistant to both paclitaxel and an anthracycline-containing chemotherapy regimen. May also be used in combination with docetaxel for the treatment of metastatic breast cancer in patients who have failed to respond to, or recurred or relasped during or following anthracycline-containing chemotherapy. …
- **Mechanism of toxicity:** Capecitabine is a prodrug that is selectively tumour-activated to its cytotoxic moiety, fluorouracil, by thymidine phosphorylase. Fluorouracil is further metabolized to two active metabolites, 5-fluoro-2-deoxyuridine monophosphate (FdUMP) and 5-fluorouridine triphosphate (FUTP), within normal and tumour cells. …
