# Orellanine

- **generic name:** Orellanine
- **T3DB:** [T3D2451](http://www.t3db.ca/toxins/T3D2451) · **PubChem:** [CID 89579](https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/compound/89579)
- **groups:** investigational

## About

Orellanine or Orellanin is a pyridine N-oxide and a crystalline alkaloid that is found naturally in some lifeforms, specifically certain fungi. It has been found in at least 34 types of mushrooms in the Cortinariaceae family.

- **Category:** Fungal Toxin, Natural Toxin
- **Route of exposure:** oral, dermal, inhalation, and parenteral (contaminated drugs).
- **Sources and uses:** Orellanine or Orellanin is a pyridine N-oxide and a crystalline alkaloid that is found naturally in some lifeforms, specifically certain fungi. It has been found in at least 34 types of mushrooms in the Cortinariaceae family.
- **Mechanism of toxicity:** Bipyridines with charged nitrogen atoms confound important redox reactions in organisms, ‘stealing’ one or two electrons and sometimes bypass the electrons into other and often undesirable redox reactions. The terminal product can be peroxide or superoxide ions, the latter of which is harmful to the cells, especially kidney cells.
- **Health effects:** The symptoms are followed by early stages of renal failure (immense thirst, frequent urination, pain on and around the kidneys) and eventually decreased or nonexistent urine output and other symptoms of renal failure occur. If left untreated death will follow.
- **Lethal dose:** In mice: 12 to 20 mg per kg body weight (leads to death within two weeks). …
- **Treatment:** Although there is no known antidote against orellanine poisoning, early hospitalization can sometimes prevent serious injury and usually prevent death. …
