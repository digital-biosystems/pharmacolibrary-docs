# Nitric acid

- **generic name:** Nitric acid
- **T3DB:** [T3D3571](http://www.t3db.ca/toxins/T3D3571) · **PubChem:** [CID 944](https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/compound/944)
- **groups:** experimental

## About

Nitric acid (HNO3), also known as aqua fortis and spirit of niter, is a highly corrosive mineral acid. The pure compound is colorless, but older samples tend to acquire a yellow cast due to decomposition into oxides of nitrogen and water. Most commercially available nitric acid has a concentration of 68%. …

- **Category:** Household Toxin, Industrial/Workplace Toxin, Synthetic Toxin
- **Route of exposure:** oral, inhalation
- **Sources and uses:** The main industrial use of nitric acid is for the production of fertilizers. Nitric acid is neutralized with ammonia to give ammonium nitrate. …
- **Mechanism of toxicity:** Nitric acid is a corrosive acid and a powerful oxidizing agent. The major hazard posed by it is chemical burns as it carries out acid hydrolysis with proteins (amide) and fats (ester) which consequently decomposes living tissue (e.g. skin and flesh). …
- **Health effects:** Skin contact with nitric acid can cause redness, pain, and severe skin burns. Nitric acid may cause severe burns to the eye and permanent eye damage. …
- **Carcinogenicity:** Ingested nitrate or nitrite under conditions that result in endogenous nitrosation is probably carcinogenic to humans (Group 2A).
- **Acute toxicity:** LD50: 138 ppm over 30 minutes (Inhalation, Rat)
- **Treatment:** The mainstay of treatment of any acid burn is copious irrigation with large amounts of tap water. …
