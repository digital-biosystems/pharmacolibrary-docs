## About

Doxorubicin is a chemotherapy drug used to treat many cancers, including breast and ovarian cancer, various lymphomas, and several leukemias. It is widely used and appears on the WHO essential medicines list; several doxorubicin products are authorised in the European Union.

<details><summary>Evidence: cardiomyopathy (15)</summary>

> “At a total cumulative doxorubicin dose of greater than 550 mg/m 2 (administered as high dose bolus injections) the incidence of chronic cardiomyopathy rapidly increases and exceeds to 1 to 5% (Von Hoff et al. 1979). Chronic cardiotoxicity is characterised clinically by congestive heart failure, with an ultimate mortality rate of 20 to 40% (Haq et al. 1985).” — [Speth 1988](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/3042244) <sub>(review)</sub>

> “manifesting mainly as heart failure usually within a year after completion of treatment, although it is widely accepted that it can manifest itself several years after treatment... studies estimate heart failure rates between 5 and 10%” — [Salazar-Mendiguchía 2014](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25001055) <sub>(review)</sub>
