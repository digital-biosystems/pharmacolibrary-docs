# Amiodarone

- **generic name:** Amiodarone
- **T3DB:** [T3D4737](http://www.t3db.ca/toxins/T3D4737) · **DrugBank:** [DB01118](https://go.drugbank.com/drugs/DB01118) · **PubChem:** [CID 2157](https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/compound/2157)
- **groups:** approved, investigational

## About

An antianginal and antiarrhythmic drug. It increases the duration of ventricular and atrial muscle action by inhibiting Na,K-activated myocardial adenosine triphosphatase. There is a resulting decrease in heart rate and in vascular resistance.

- **Category:** Drug, Synthetic Toxin
- **Route of exposure:** slow and variable (about 20 to 55% of an oral dose is absorbed).
- **Sources and uses:** Intravenously, for initiation of treatment and prophylaxis of frequently recurring ventricular fibrillation and hemodynamically unstable ventricular tachycardia in patients refractory to other therapy. Orally, for the treatment of life-threatening recurrent ventricular arrhythmias such as recurrent ventricular fibrillation and recurrent hemodynamically unstable ventricular tachycardia.
- **Mechanism of toxicity:** The antiarrhythmic effect of amiodarone may be due to at least two major actions. It prolongs the myocardial cell-action potential (phase 3) duration and refractory period and acts as a noncompetitive a- and b-adrenergic inhibitor.
- **Health effects:** Some side effects have a significant mortality rate: specifically, hepatitis, exacerbation of asthma and congestive failure, and pneumonitis.
- **Acute toxicity:** Intravenous, mouse: LD<sub>50</sub> = 178 mg/kg.
