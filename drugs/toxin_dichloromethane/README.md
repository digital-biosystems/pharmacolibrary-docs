# Dichloromethane

- **generic name:** Dichloromethane
- **T3DB:** [T3D0080](http://www.t3db.ca/toxins/T3D0080) · **PubChem:** [CID 6344](https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/compound/6344)

## About

Dichloromethane (DCM, methylene chloride, or methylene bichloride) is an organochlorine compound and a dihalomethane with the formula CH2Cl2. This colorless, volatile liquid with a chloroform-like, sweet odor is widely used as a solvent. Although it is not miscible with water, it is slightly polar and miscible with many organic solvents.

<p><small>Source: <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dichloromethane?oldid=1385652732">Wikipedia</a> · <a href="https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/">CC BY-SA 4.0</a></small></p>

- **Category:** Pesticide, Household Toxin, Industrial/Workplace Toxin, Pollutant, Airborne Pollutant, Food Toxin, Synthetic Toxin
- **Route of exposure:** oral, inhalation, dermal
- **Sources and uses:** Methylene chloride is widely used as a solvent in industrial processes, paint stripper, and degreaser. It is also used in food preparation, aerosol propellants, pesticides, and the manufacture of photographic film.
- **Mechanism of toxicity:** Methylene chloride targets the lungs, blood system, and nervous system. In the lungs its metabolites damage Clara cells. …
- **Health effects:** Exposure to methylene chloride may cause optic neuropathy and hepatitis. Very high concentrations can lead to unconciousness, coma, and death. …
- **Lethal dose:** 357 mg/kg (oral) or 50 000 ppm (inhalation) for an adult human.
- **Carcinogenicity:** IARC group 2A, probably carcinogenic to humans.
- **Minimal risk levels (ATSDR):** Acute Inhalation: 0.6 ppm
Intermediate Inhalation: 0.3 ppm
Chronic Inhalation: 0.3 ppm
Acute Oral: 0.2 mg/kg/day
Chronic Oral: 0.06 mg/kg/day
- **Acute toxicity:** LD50: 437 mg/kg (Intraperitoneal, Mouse)
LD50: 6460 mg/kg (Subcutaneous, Mouse)
LD50: 1600 mg/kg (Oral, Rat)
LC50: 14 400 ppm over 7 hours (Inhalation, Mouse)
- **Treatment:** Treatment of methylene chloride exposure is mainly symptomatic. …
