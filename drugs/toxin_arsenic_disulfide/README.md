# Arsenic disulfide

- **generic name:** Arsenic disulfide
- **T3DB:** [T3D0277](http://www.t3db.ca/toxins/T3D0277) · **PubChem:** [CID 6328537](https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/compound/6328537)

## About

Arsenic disulfide is a sulfide of arsenic that occurs as the mineral realgar. Arsenic is a chemical element that has the symbol As and atomic number 33. It is a poisonous metalloid that has many allotropic forms: yellow (molecular non-metallic) and several black and grey forms (metalloids) are a few that are seen. …

- **Category:** Industrial/Workplace Toxin, Pollutant, Airborne Pollutant, Synthetic Toxin
- **Route of exposure:** oral, inhalation, dermal
- **Sources and uses:** Arsenic disulfide (As), also known as red orpiment and ruby arsenic, is used as a pigment in the manufacture of fireworks and paints.
- **Mechanism of toxicity:** Arsenic and its metabolites disrupt ATP production through several mechanisms. At the level of the citric acid cycle, arsenic inhibits pyruvate dehydrogenase and by competing with phosphate it uncouples oxidative phosphorylation, thus inhibiting energy-linked reduction of NAD+, mitochondrial respiration, and ATP synthesis. …
- **Health effects:** Arsenic poisoning can lead to death from multi-system organ failure, probably from necrotic cell death, not apoptosis. Arsenic is also a known carcinogen, esepcially in skin, liver, bladder and lung cancers.
- **Carcinogenicity:** IARC group 1, carcinogenic to humans.
- **Minimal risk levels (ATSDR):** Acute Oral: 0.005 mg/kg/day 
Chronic Oral: 0.0003 mg/kg/day 
Chronic Inhalation: 0.01 mg/m3
