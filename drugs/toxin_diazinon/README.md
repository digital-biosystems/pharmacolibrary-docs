# Diazinon

- **generic name:** Diazinon
- **T3DB:** [T3D0056](http://www.t3db.ca/toxins/T3D0056) · **PubChem:** [CID 3017](https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/compound/3017)

## About

Nonsystemic insecticide for rice and fruit trees. Cholinesterase inhibitor. Diazinon is used against animal ectoparasites Diazinon has been shown to exhibit apoptotic and antibiotic functions.

- **Category:** Pesticide, Household Toxin, Food Toxin, Synthetic Toxin
- **Route of exposure:** oral, inhalation, dermal
- **Sources and uses:** Diazinon is used as an insecticide in agriculture, mainly on fruit and vegetable field crops.
- **Mechanism of toxicity:** Diazinon is a cholinesterase or acetylcholinesterase (AChE) inhibitor. A cholinesterase inhibitor (or 'anticholinesterase') suppresses the action of acetylcholinesterase. …
- **Health effects:** Acute exposure to cholinesterase inhibitors can cause a cholinergic crisis characterized by severe nausea/vomiting, salivation, sweating, bradycardia, hypotension, collapse, and convulsions. Increasing muscle weakness is a possibility and may result in death if respiratory muscles are involved. …
- **Lethal dose:** 25 g for an adult human.
- **Carcinogenicity:** Spraying and application of nonarsenical insecticides entail exposures that are probably carcinogenic to humans (Group 2A).
- **Minimal risk levels (ATSDR):** Intermediate Inhalation: 0.01 mg/m3
Acute Oral: 0.006 mg/kg/day
Intermediate Oral: 0.002 mg/kg/day
Chronic Oral: 0.0007 mg/kg/day
- **Acute toxicity:** LD50: 66 mg/kg (Oral, Rat)
LD50: 180 mg/kg (Dermal, Rat)
LD50: 65 mg/kg (Intraperitoneal, Rat)
LD50: 58 mg/kg (Subcutaneous, Mouse)
LD50: 180 mg/kg (Intravenous, Mouse)
- **Treatment:** If the compound has been ingested, rapid gastric lavage should be performed using 5% sodium bicarbonate. …
