# 1,2-Dichlorobenzene

- **generic name:** 1,2-Dichlorobenzene
- **T3DB:** [T3D0172](http://www.t3db.ca/toxins/T3D0172) · **PubChem:** [CID 7239](https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/compound/7239)
- **groups:** approved, withdrawn

## About

1,2-Dichlorobenzene (1,2-DCB), or ortho-dichlorobenzene, is an organic compound with the formula C6H4Cl2. This colourless liquid is poorly soluble in water but miscible with most organic solvents. 1,2-Dichlorobenzene contains two chlorine atoms connected to one benzene molecule. Most of the 1,2-DCB released into the environment is present as a vapor. 1,2-DCB can burn, but they do not burn easily.

- **Category:** Household Toxin, Industrial/Workplace Toxin, Pollutant, Food Toxin, Synthetic Toxin
- **Route of exposure:** inhalation, oral, dermal
- **Sources and uses:** Exposure may result from breathing contaminated air, drinking contaminated water or eating contaminated food, and skin contact.
- **Mechanism of toxicity:** 1,2-DCB was found to covalently bind to DNA, RNA, and proteins of liver, kidney, lung, and stomach.
- **Health effects:** Inhaling the vapor or dusts of 1,2-DCB at very high concentrations could be very irritating to eyes and nose and cause burning and tearing of the eyes, coughing, difficult breathing, and an upset stomach. Ingestion of 1,2-DCB can cause effects in the kidneys and blood, and that 1,3-DCB caused thyroid and pituitary effects.
- **Carcinogenicity:** IARC group 3, not classifiable as to its carcinogenicity to humans.
- **Minimal risk levels (ATSDR):** Acute Oral: 0.7 mg/kg/day (Rat and Mouse) 
Intermediate Oral: 0.6 mg/kg/day (Rat and Mouse) 
Chronic Oral: 0.3 mg/kg/day (Rat and Mouse)
- **Acute toxicity:** LD50: 500 mg/kg (Oral, Rat)
- **Treatment:** Following oral exposure, administer charcoal as a slurry; administer oxygen to all cyanotic or symptomatic patients. …
