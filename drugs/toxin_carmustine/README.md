## About

Carmustine is a cell-cycle phase nonspecific alkylating antineoplastic agent. It is used in the treatment of brain tumors and various other malignant neoplasms. This substance may reasonably be anticipated to be a carcinogen according to the Fourth Annual Report on Carcinogens (NTP 85-002, 1985).

- **Category:** Drug, Synthetic Toxin
- **Sources and uses:** For the treatment of brain tumors, multiple myeloma, Hodgkin's disease and Non-Hodgkin's lymphomas.
- **Mechanism of toxicity:** Carmustine causes cross-links in DNA and RNA, leading to the inhibition of DNA synthesis, RNA production and RNA translation (protein synthesis). Carmustine also binds to and modifies (carbamoylates) glutathione reductase. …
- **Carcinogenicity:** IARC group 2A, probably carcinogenic to humans.
- **Acute toxicity:** The oral LD<sub>50</sub>s in rat and mouse are 20 mg/kg and 45 mg/kg, respectively.
