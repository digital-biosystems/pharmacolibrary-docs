# Nitrofurantoin

- **generic name:** Nitrofurantoin
- **T3DB:** [T3D2679](http://www.t3db.ca/toxins/T3D2679) · **DrugBank:** [DB00698](https://go.drugbank.com/drugs/DB00698) · **PubChem:** [CID 5353830](https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/compound/5353830)
- **groups:** approved, investigational, vet_approved

## About

A bacteriostatic or bactericidal agent depending on the concentration and susceptibility of the infecting organism. Nitrofurantoin is active against some gram positive organisms such as S. aureus, S. epidermidis, S. saprophyticus, Enterococcus faecalis, S. agalactiae, group D streptococci, viridians streptococci and Corynebacterium. Its spectrum of activity against gram negative organisms includes E. coli, Enterobacter, Neisseria, Salmonella and Shigella. …

- **Category:** Drug, Synthetic Toxin
- **Route of exposure:** oral.
Readily absorbed in GI tract primarily in small intestine. Enhanced by food or delayed gastric emptying via enhanced dissolution rate of the drug.
- **Sources and uses:** May be used as an alternative in the treatment of urinary tract infections. May be used by females pericoitally for prophylaxis against recurrent cystitis related to coitus.
- **Mechanism of toxicity:** Nitrofurantoin is activated by bacterial flavoproteins (nitrofuran reductase) to active reduced reactive intermediates that are thought to modulate and damage ribosomal proteins or other macromolecules, especially DNA, causing inhibition of DNA, RNA, protein, and cell wall synthesis. Nitrofurantoin inhibits bacterial acetyl-coenzyme A, interfering with the organism's carbohydrate metabolism. …
- **Health effects:** Sialadenitis, pancreatitis, peripheral neuropathy, Asthenia, vertigo, and nystagmus, benign intracranial hypertension (pseudotumor cerebri), confusion, depression, optic neuritis, and psychotic reactions.
- **Carcinogenicity:** IARC group 3, not classifiable as to its carcinogenicity to humans.
- **Treatment:** There is no specific antidote, but a high fluid intake should be maintained to promote urinary excretion of the drug. …
