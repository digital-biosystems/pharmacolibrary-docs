# Cyanocobalamin

- **generic name:** Cyanocobalamin
- **T3DB:** [T3D0673](http://www.t3db.ca/toxins/T3D0673) · **DrugBank:** [DB00115](https://go.drugbank.com/drugs/DB00115) · **PubChem:** [CID 44176380](https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/compound/44176380)
- **groups:** approved, investigational, nutraceutical

## About

Cyanocobalamin (commonly known as Vitamin B12) is the most chemically complex of all the vitamins. Cyanocobalamin's structure is based on a corrin ring, which, although similar to the porphyrin ring found in heme, chlorophyll, and cytochrome, has two of the pyrrole rings directly bonded. The central metal ion is Co (cobalt). …

- **Category:** Drug, Pollutant, Airborne Pollutant, Food Toxin, Natural Toxin
- **Route of exposure:** inhalation, oral, dermal
Readily absorbed in the lower half of the ileum.
- **Sources and uses:** Cyanocobalamin is a form of vitamin B12. It is used for treatment of pernicious anemia (due to lack of or inhibition of intrinsic factor) and for prevention and treatment of vitamin B 12 deficiency.
- **Mechanism of toxicity:** Vitamin B12 is used in the body in two forms: Methylcobalamin and 5-deoxyadenosyl cobalamin. The enzyme methionine synthase needs methylcobalamin as a cofactor. …
- **Health effects:** Exposure to high amount of cobalt can cause heart, lung, kidney, and liver damage. Skin contact is known to result in contact dermatitis. …
- **Carcinogenicity:** IARC group 2B, possibly carcinogenic to humans.
- **Minimal risk levels (ATSDR):** Chronic Inhalation: 0.0001 mg/m3; Intermediate Oral: 0.01 mg/kg/day
- **Acute toxicity:** ORL-MUS LD<sub>50</sub> > 8000 mg/kg
- **Treatment:** Treatment of cobalt poisoning is symptomatic.
- **Detected in:** blood, cerebrospinal fluid, urine
