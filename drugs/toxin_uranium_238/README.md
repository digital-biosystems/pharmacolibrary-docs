# Uranium-238

- **generic name:** Uranium-238
- **T3DB:** [T3D0791](http://www.t3db.ca/toxins/T3D0791) · **PubChem:** [CID 23989](https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/compound/23989)

## About

Uranium is a chemical element; it has symbol U and atomic number 92. It is a silvery-grey metal in the actinide series of the periodic table. A uranium atom has 92 protons and 92 electrons, of which 6 are valence electrons. Uranium radioactively decays, usually by emitting an alpha particle. The half-life of this decay varies between 159,200 and 4.5 billion years for different isotopes, making them useful for dating the age of the Earth. The most common isotopes in natural uranium are uranium-238 (which has 146 neutrons and accounts for over 99% of uranium on Earth) and uranium-235 (which has 143 neutrons). Uranium has the highest atomic weight of the primordially occurring elements. Its density is about 70% higher than that of lead and slightly lower than that of gold or tungsten. It occurs naturally in low concentrations of a few parts per million in soil, rock and water, and is commercially extracted from uranium-bearing minerals such as uraninite.

<p><small>Source: <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uranium?oldid=1384663420">Wikipedia</a> · <a href="https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/">CC BY-SA 4.0</a></small></p>

- **Category:** Industrial/Workplace Toxin, Pollutant, Airborne Pollutant, Natural Toxin
- **Route of exposure:** oral, inhalation, dermal, radiation
- **Sources and uses:** Uranium-238 is used in the military sector for kinetic energy penetrators and armor plating. Uranium is also used as a colorant in uranium glass, producing orange-red to lemon yellow hues.
- **Mechanism of toxicity:** Uranium is combined with either bicarbonate or a plasma protein in the blood but once in the kidney, it is released and forms complexes with phosphate ligands and proteins in the tubular wall, causing damage. Uranium may also inhibit both sodium transport-dependent and independent ATP utilization and mitochondrial oxidative phosphorylation in the renal proximal tubule. …
- **Health effects:** Uranium primarily damages the kidney, but may also damage the lungs, central nervous system, and immune system. Uranium's radioactivity is believed to damage the DNA, resulting in carcinogenic effects and reproductive and developmental damage.
- **Carcinogenicity:** IARC group 1, carcinogenic to humans.
- **Minimal risk levels (ATSDR):** Intermediate Inhalation: 0.0004 mg/m3 (Soluble uranium salts)
Chronic Inhalation: 0.0003 mg/m3 (Soluble uranium salts)
Intermediate Oral: 0.002 mg/kg/day (Soluble uranium salts)
Intermediate Inhalation: 0.008 mg/m3 (Insoluble uranium compounds)
