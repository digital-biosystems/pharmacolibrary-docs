# Telithromycin

## About

Telithromycin, a semi-synthetic erythromycin derivative, belongs to a new chemical class of antibiotics called ketolides. Ketolides have been recently added to the macrolide-lincosamide-streptogramin class of antibiotics. Similar to the macrolide antibiotics, telithromycin prevents bacterial growth by interfering with bacterial protein synthesis. …

- **Category:** Drug, Synthetic Toxin
- **Route of exposure:** absolute bioavailability is approximately 57%. Maximal concentrations are reached 0.5 - 4 hours following oral administration. Food intake does not affected absorption.
- **Sources and uses:** For the treatment of <i>Pneumococcal</i> infection, acute sinusitis, acute bacterial tonsillitis, acute bronchitis and bronchiolitis, lower respiratory tract infection and lobar (pneumococcal) pneumonia.
- **Mechanism of toxicity:** Telithromycin acts by binding to domains II and V of 23S rRNA of the 50S ribosomal subunit. By binding at domain II, telithromycin retains activity against gram-positive cocci (e.g., <i>Streptococcus pneumoniae</i>) in the presence of resistance mediated by methylases (erm genes) that alter the domain V binding site of telithromycin. …
- **Acute toxicity:** LD50>2000 mg/kg (PO in rats).
