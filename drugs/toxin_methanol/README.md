# Methanol

- **generic name:** Methanol
- **T3DB:** [T3D0771](http://www.t3db.ca/toxins/T3D0771) · **PubChem:** [CID 887](https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/compound/887)

## About

Methanol (also called methyl alcohol, wood alcohol, and wood spirit, amongst other names) is an organic chemical compound and the simplest aliphatic alcohol, with the chemical formula CH3OH (a methyl group linked to a hydroxyl group, often abbreviated as MeOH). It is a light, volatile, colorless and flammable liquid with a distinctive alcoholic odor similar to that of ethanol (potable alcohol), but is more acutely toxic than the latter.

<p><small>Source: <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Methanol?oldid=1380006541">Wikipedia</a> · <a href="https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/">CC BY-SA 4.0</a></small></p>

- **Category:** Household Toxin, Industrial/Workplace Toxin, Pollutant, Airborne Pollutant, Food Toxin, Natural Toxin
- **Route of exposure:** oral
- **Sources and uses:** Methanol is found in a host of consumer products, including windshield washer fluid, and is used in the manufacture of formaldehyde.
- **Mechanism of toxicity:** The target of methanol in the eye is the retina, specifically the optic disk and optic nerve. Muller cells and rod and cone cells are altered functionally and structurally, because cytochrome oxidase activity in mitochondria is inhibited, resulting in a reduction in ATP.
- **Health effects:** Acute methanol poisoning in humans is characterized by an asymptomatic period of 12h to 24h followed by formic acidemia, ocular toxicity, coma, and in extreme cases death. Visual disturbances develop between 18h to 48h after ingestion and range from mild photophobia and blurred vision to markedly reduced visual acuity and complete blindness.
- **Detected in:** blood, breast milk, cerebrospinal fluid, saliva, urine
