# Hydrogen fluoride

- **generic name:** Hydrogen fluoride
- **T3DB:** [T3D0247](http://www.t3db.ca/toxins/T3D0247) · **PubChem:** [CID 14917](https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/compound/14917)
- **groups:** approved

## About

HF can refer to both hydrogen fluoride (gas) and hydrofluoric acid. Hydrofluoric acid is a solution of hydrogen fluoride (HF) in water. It is a precursor to almost all fluorine compounds, including pharmaceuticals such as fluoxetine (Prozac), surface coatings such as PTFE (Teflon), and elemental fluorine itself. …

- **Category:** Household Toxin, Industrial/Workplace Toxin, Pollutant, Airborne Pollutant, Synthetic Toxin
- **Route of exposure:** oral, inhalation, dermal
- **Sources and uses:** Hydrofluoric acid is used mainly for etching glass. It is also used in oil refining and as a precursor to other fluoride compounds.
- **Mechanism of toxicity:** In addition to being a highly corrosive liquid, hydrofluoric acid is also a contact poison. As with most acids HF can cause tissue burns through the denaturation of proteins and partial hydrolysis of proteins. …
- **Health effects:** Hydrogen fluoride is extremely corrosive. It may penetrate the skin and weaken the bones, as well as interfere with nerve function and react with blood calcium, causing cardiac arrest.
- **Lethal dose:** 50 to 250 ppm over 5 minutes (Inhalation) or 1.5 grams (Oral) for an adult human.
- **Acute toxicity:** LC50: 500 ppm over 1 hours (Inhalation, Mouse)
- **Treatment:** Hydrofluoric acid exposure is often treated with calcium gluconate, a source of Ca2+ that sequesters the fluoride ions. …
