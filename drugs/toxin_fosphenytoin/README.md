# Fosphenytoin

- **generic name:** Fosphenytoin
- **T3DB:** [T3D3036](http://www.t3db.ca/toxins/T3D3036) · **DrugBank:** [DB01320](https://go.drugbank.com/drugs/DB01320) · **PubChem:** [CID 56339](https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/compound/56339)
- **groups:** approved, investigational

## About

Fosphenytoin is a water-soluble phenytoin prodrug used only in hospitals for the treatment of epileptic seizures. It works by slowing down impulses in the brain that cause seizures. Its main mechanism is to block frequency-dependent, use-dependent and voltage-dependent neuronal sodium channels, and therefore limit repetitive firing of action potentials.

- **Category:** Drug, Synthetic Toxin
- **Route of exposure:** fosphenytoin is completely bioavailable following lM administration.
- **Sources and uses:** For the control of generalized convulsive status epilepticus and prevention and treatment of seizures occurring during neurosurgery. It can also be substituted, short-term, for oral phenytoin.
- **Mechanism of toxicity:** Fosphenytoin is a prodrug of phenytoin and accordingly, its anticonvulsant effects are attributable to phenytoin. Phenytoin acts on sodium channels on the neuronal cell membrane, limiting the spread of seizure activity and reducing seizure propagation. …
- **Health effects:** May cause a potentially dangerous rash that may develop into Stevens Johnson syndrome, an extremely rare but potentially fatal skin disease.
- **Acute toxicity:** LD50: 156 mg PE/kg (Intravenous, Mouse)
LD50: 250 mg PE/kg (Intravenous, Rat)
- **Treatment:** Treatment is nonspecific since there is no known antidote to Fosphenytoin overdosage. …
