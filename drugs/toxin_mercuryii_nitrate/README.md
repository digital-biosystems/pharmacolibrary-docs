# Mercury(II) nitrate

- **generic name:** Mercury(II) nitrate
- **T3DB:** [T3D0354](http://www.t3db.ca/toxins/T3D0354) · **PubChem:** [CID 115042](https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/compound/115042)

## About

Mercury(II) nitrate is an inorganic compound with the chemical formula Hg(NO3)2. It is the mercury(II) salt of nitric acid HNO3. No evidence for any neutral mercury(II) nitrate has been reported. Commonly used and commercially available 'mercury(II) nitrate monohydrate' is actually a basic mercury nitrate with the formula Hg(NO3)OH·nH2O. All mercury(II) nitrate salts are colorless or white soluble crystalline solids that are occasionally used as reagents in organic synthesis.

<p><small>Source: <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mercury%28II%29_nitrate?oldid=1380026576">Wikipedia</a> · <a href="https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/">CC BY-SA 4.0</a></small></p>

- **Category:** Industrial/Workplace Toxin, Pollutant, Airborne Pollutant, Synthetic Toxin
- **Route of exposure:** oral, inhalation, dermal
- **Sources and uses:** Mercury(II) nitrate is used mainly in the synthesis of other mercuric compounds.
- **Mechanism of toxicity:** High-affinity binding of the divalent mercuric ion to thiol or sulfhydryl groups of proteins is believed to be the major mechanism for the activity of mercury. Through alterations in intracellular thiol status, mercury can promote oxidative stress, lipid peroxidation, mitochondrial dysfunction, and changes in heme metabolism. …
- **Health effects:** Mercury mainly affects the nervous system. Exposure to high levels of metallic, inorganic, or organic mercury can permanently damage the brain, kidneys, and developing fetus. …
- **Carcinogenicity:** Ingested nitrate or nitrite under conditions that result in endogenous nitrosation is probably carcinogenic to humans (Group 2A). …
- **Minimal risk levels (ATSDR):** Chronic Inhalation: 0.0002 mg/m3
- **Acute toxicity:** LD50: 26 mg/kg (Oral, Rat); LD50: 75 mg/kg (Dermal, Rat); LD50: 7 mg/kg (Intraperitoneal, Mouse)
