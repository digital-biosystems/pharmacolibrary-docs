# Naphthalene

## About

Naphthalene is the simplest polyaromatic hydrocarbon (PAH) consisting of two fused benzene rings. It has a distinct, pungent odor that can be detected at levels as low as 0.08 ppm. Naphthalene is the most abundant single component of coal tar so most of it is now industrially derived from coal tar. …

- **Category:** Household Toxin, Industrial/Workplace Toxin, Pollutant, Airborne Pollutant, Food Toxin, Natural Toxin
- **Route of exposure:** oral, inhalation
- **Sources and uses:** Industrially, naphthalene is used in the production of phthalic anhydride, as a solvent for chemical reactions, as a wetting agent and as a fumigant. Naphthalene is produced industrially via coal tar and naturally with trace amounts of naphthalene being produced by black walnuts, magnolias and specific types of deer.
- **Mechanism of toxicity:** PAH's such as naphthalene are transported throughout the body after binding blood proteins such as albumin. Binding to the aryl hydrocarbon receptor or glycine N-methyltransferase induces the expression of cytochrome P450 enzymes (especially CYP1A1, CYP1A2, and CYP1B1). …
- **Health effects:** Inhalation of naphthalene vapor has been associated with headaches, nausea, vomiting and dizziness. Hemolysis, the abnormal breakdown of red blood cells, may occur following ingestion or sufficient dermal exposure to either naphthalene or to naphthalene-treated fabric. …
- **Carcinogenicity:** IARC group 2B, possibly carcinogenic to humans.
- **Acute toxicity:** LD50: 490 mg/kg (Oral, Rat)
LD50: >20 g/kg (Dermal, Rabbit)
LD50: 150 mg/kg (Intraperitoneal, Mouse)
LD50: 969 mg/kg (Subcutaneous, Mouse)
LD50: 100 mg/kg (Intravenous, Mouse)
- **Treatment:** There is no known antidote for PAHs. …
