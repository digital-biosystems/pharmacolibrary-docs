# Cobalt sulfate

- **generic name:** Cobalt sulfate
- **T3DB:** [T3D0669](http://www.t3db.ca/toxins/T3D0669) · **PubChem:** [CID 24965](https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/compound/24965)
- **groups:** investigational

## About

Cobalt sulfate is found in alcoholic beverages. Once used in fermented malt beverages as a foam stabiliser; now prohibited from use in food<br /> Cobalt sulfate belongs to the family of Transition Metal Sulfates. These are inorganic compounds in which the largest oxoanion is sulfate, and in which the heaviest atom not in an oxoanion is a transition metal.

- **Category:** Industrial/Workplace Toxin, Pollutant, Airborne Pollutant, Food Toxin, Synthetic Toxin
- **Route of exposure:** inhalation, oral, dermal
- **Sources and uses:** Cobalt(II) sulfate is used in pigments for glass and porcelain, as well as in storage batteries and electroplating baths, sympathetic inks, and as an additive to soils and animal feeds.
- **Mechanism of toxicity:** Cobalt is believed to exhibit its toxicity through a oxidant-based and free radical-based processes. It produces oxygen radicals and may be oxidized to ionic cobalt, causing increased lipid peroxidation, DNA damage, and inducing certain enzymes that lead to cell apoptosis. …
- **Health effects:** Exposure to high amount of cobalt can cause heart, lung, kidney, and liver damage. Skin contact is known to result in contact dermatitis. …
- **Carcinogenicity:** IARC group 2B, possibly carcinogenic to humans.
- **Minimal risk levels (ATSDR):** Chronic Inhalation: 0.0001 mg/m3; Intermediate Oral: 0.01 mg/kg/day
- **Acute toxicity:** LD50: 424 mg/kg (Oral, Rat); LD50: 143 mg/kg (Intraperitoneal, Mouse); LD50: 18 200 ug/kg (Intravenous, Rat)
- **Treatment:** Treatment of cobalt poisoning is symptomatic.
- **Detected in:** blood, cerebrospinal fluid, saliva, urine
