# Nickel hydroxide

- **generic name:** Nickel hydroxide
- **T3DB:** [T3D0693](http://www.t3db.ca/toxins/T3D0693) · **PubChem:** [CID 61534](https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/compound/61534)

## About

Nickel(II) hydroxide is the inorganic compound with the formula Ni(OH)2. It is a lime-green solid that dissolves with decomposition in ammonia and amines and is attacked by acids. It is electroactive, being converted to the Ni(III) oxy-hydroxide, leading to widespread applications in rechargeable batteries.

<p><small>Source: <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nickel%28II%29_hydroxide?oldid=1380041694">Wikipedia</a> · <a href="https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/">CC BY-SA 4.0</a></small></p>

- **Category:** Industrial/Workplace Toxin, Pollutant, Airborne Pollutant, Natural Toxin
- **Route of exposure:** inhalation, oral, dermal
- **Sources and uses:** Nickel hydroxide is used in rechargeable battery electrodes.
- **Mechanism of toxicity:** Nickel is known to substitute for other essential elements in certain enzmes, such as calcineurin. It is genotoxic, and some nickel compounds have been shown to promote cell proliferation. …
- **Health effects:** The most common harmful health effect of nickel in humans is an allergic reaction. This usually manifests as a skin rash, although some people experience asthma attacks. …
- **Carcinogenicity:** IARC group 1, carcinogenic to humans.
- **Minimal risk levels (ATSDR):** Intermediate Inhalation: 0.0002 mg/m3; Chronic Inhalation: 0.00009 mg/m3
- **Acute toxicity:** LD50: 60 mg/kg (Subcutaneous, Mouse)
