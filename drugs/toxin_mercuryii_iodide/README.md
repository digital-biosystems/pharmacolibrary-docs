# Mercury(II) iodide

- **generic name:** Mercury(II) iodide
- **T3DB:** [T3D0350](http://www.t3db.ca/toxins/T3D0350) · **DrugBank:** [DB04445](https://go.drugbank.com/drugs/DB04445) · **PubChem:** [CID 24485](https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/compound/24485)
- **groups:** experimental

## About

Mercury(II) iodide is the inorganic compound with the molecular formula HgI2. The compound has attracted interest because it exhibits thermochromism, i.e. its color changes markedly with temperature. It is typically produced synthetically but can also be found in nature as the extremely rare mineral coccinite. Unlike the related mercury(II) chloride, it is poorly soluble in water (<100 ppm).

<p><small>Source: <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mercury%28II%29_iodide?oldid=1383943906">Wikipedia</a> · <a href="https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/">CC BY-SA 4.0</a></small></p>

- **Category:** Industrial/Workplace Toxin, Pollutant, Airborne Pollutant, Natural Toxin
- **Route of exposure:** oral, inhalation, dermal
- **Sources and uses:** Mercury(II) iodide is aa semiconductor and also used in x-ray and gamma ray detection, as well as veterinary medicine.
- **Mechanism of toxicity:** High-affinity binding of the divalent mercuric ion to thiol or sulfhydryl groups of proteins is believed to be the major mechanism for the activity of mercury. Through alterations in intracellular thiol status, mercury can promote oxidative stress, lipid peroxidation, mitochondrial dysfunction, and changes in heme metabolism. …
- **Health effects:** Mercury mainly affects the nervous system. Exposure to high levels of metallic, inorganic, or organic mercury can permanently damage the brain, kidneys, and developing fetus. …
- **Carcinogenicity:** IARC group 3, not classifiable as to its carcinogenicity to humans.
- **Minimal risk levels (ATSDR):** Chronic Inhalation: 0.0002 mg/m3
- **Acute toxicity:** LD50: 18 mg/kg (Oral, Rat); LD50: 4.2 mg/kg (Intraperitoneal, Rat)
