# Patulin

- **generic name:** Patulin
- **T3DB:** [T3D3661](http://www.t3db.ca/toxins/T3D3661) · **PubChem:** [CID 4696](https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/compound/4696)
- **groups:** investigational

## About

Patulin is found in pomes. Mycotoxin, found as a contaminant of foods, e.g. apple juice. Sometimes detd. in apple juice Patulin is a mycotoxin produced by a variety of molds, particularly Aspergillus and Penicillium. …

- **Category:** Industrial/Workplace Toxin, Fungal Toxin, Food Toxin, Natural Toxin
- **Route of exposure:** oral, dermal, inhalation, and parenteral (contaminated drugs).
- **Sources and uses:** Patulin is a mycotoxin produced by a variety of molds, particularly Aspergillus and Penicillium. Although patulin can occur in many molding fruits, grains and other foods, the major source of patulin contamination is in apples with brown rot, and in apple cider or apple juice. …
- **Mechanism of toxicity:** Patulin's strong affinity to sulfhydryl groups enables to to inhibit a number of enzymes, including succinate dehydrogenase, alcohol dehydrogenases, ATPases, acetylcholinesterase, aldolases, protein tyrosine phosphatases, RNA polymerases, aminoacyl-tRNA synthetase, ribonuclease H, and lactate dehydrogenase. This widespread enzyme inhibition has a number of effects, including inhibition of aerobic respiration, inhibition of RNA synthesis, and inhibition of protein synthesis. …
- **Health effects:** Animals studies have shown patulin to cause gastrointestinal hyperemia, distension, hemorrhage and ulceration. It has also been shown to be immunotoxic, and neurotoxic.
- **Carcinogenicity:** IARC group 3, not classifiable as to its carcinogenicity to humans.
- **Minimal risk levels (ATSDR):** The World Health Organization recommends a maximum concentration of 50 µg/kg (50 ppb) in juices.
- **Acute toxicity:** LD50: 29-48 mg/kg (Oral, Mouse) 
LD50: 10 mg/kg (Subcutaneous, Mosue) 
LD50: 5.7-8.17 mg/kg (Intraperitoneal, Mouse) 
LD50: 8.57 mg/kg (Intravenous, Mouse)
- **Treatment:** Symptomatic and supportive care is the mainstay of therapy.
