# Zinc chloride

- **generic name:** Zinc chloride
- **T3DB:** [T3D0726](http://www.t3db.ca/toxins/T3D0726) · **PubChem:** [CID 5727](https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/compound/5727)
- **groups:** approved

## About

Zinc chloride is a nutrient supplement A number of salts containing the tetrachlorozincate anion, ZnCl2 4, are known. Caulton's reagent, V2Cl3(thf)6Zn2Cl6 is an example of a salt containing Zn2Cl2 6. The compound Cs3ZnCl5 contains tetrahedral ZnCl2 4 and Cl anions. …

- **Category:** Household Toxin, Industrial/Workplace Toxin, Food Toxin, Synthetic Toxin
- **Route of exposure:** inhalation, oral, dermal
- **Sources and uses:** Zinc chloride is used in textile processing, metallurgical fluxes, and chemical synthesis.
- **Mechanism of toxicity:** Anaemia results from the excessive absorption of zinc suppressing copper and iron absorption, most likely through competitive binding of intestinal mucosal cells. Unbalanced levels of copper and zinc binding to Cu,Zn-superoxide dismutase has been linked to amyotrophic lateral sclerosis (ALS). …
- **Health effects:** Chronic exposure to zinc causes anemia, atazia, lethargy, and decreases the level of good cholesterol in the body. It is also believed to cause pancreatic and reproductive damage.
- **Minimal risk levels (ATSDR):** Intermediate Oral: 0.3 mg/kg/day 
Chronic Oral: 0.3 mg/kg/day
- **Acute toxicity:** LD50: 350 mg/kg (Oral, Rat); LD50: 58 mg/kg (Intraperitoneal, Rat); LD50: 330 mg/kg (Subcutaneous, Mouse)
- **Treatment:** Zinc poisoning is treated symptomatically, often by administering fluids such as water or milk, or with gastric lavage.
- **Detected in:** blood, cerebrospinal fluid, saliva, urine
