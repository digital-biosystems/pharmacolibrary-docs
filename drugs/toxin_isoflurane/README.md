# Isoflurane

- **generic name:** Isoflurane
- **T3DB:** [T3D2882](http://www.t3db.ca/toxins/T3D2882) · **DrugBank:** [DB00753](https://go.drugbank.com/drugs/DB00753) · **PubChem:** [CID 3763](https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/compound/3763)
- **groups:** approved, investigational, vet_approved

## About

Isoflurane is only found in individuals that have used or taken this drug. It is a stable, non-explosive inhalation anesthetic, relatively free from significant side effects. [PubChem]Isoflurane induces a reduction in junctional conductance by decreasing gap junction channel opening times and increasing gap junction channel closing times. Isoflurane also activates calcium dependent ATPase in the sarcoplasmic reticulum by increasing the fluidity of the lipid membrane. …

- **Category:** Drug, Household Toxin, Synthetic Toxin
- **Route of exposure:** inhalation
- **Sources and uses:** For induction and maintenance of general anesthesia.
- **Mechanism of toxicity:** Isoflurane induces a reduction in junctional conductance by decreasing gap junction channel opening times and increasing gap junction channel closing times. Isoflurane also activates calcium dependent ATPase in the sarcoplasmic reticulum by increasing the fluidity of the lipid membrane. …
- **Health effects:** May lead to cardiac arrhythmias and death (Rarely). In susceptible individuals, Isoflurane anesthesia may trigger a skeletal muscle hypermetabolic state leading to high oxygen demand and the clinical syndrome known as malignant hyperthermia. …
- **Acute toxicity:** LC50=15300 ppm/3 hrs (inhalation by rat)
- **Treatment:** Stop drug administration, establish a clear airway, and initiate assisted or controlled ventilation with pure oxygen.
