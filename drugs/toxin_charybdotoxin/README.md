# Charybdotoxin

- **generic name:** Charybdotoxin
- **T3DB:** [T3D2507](http://www.t3db.ca/toxins/T3D2507) · **PubChem:** [CID 44134622](https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/compound/44134622)

## About

Charybdotoxin is a peptide toxin produced by the Yellow scorpion (Leiurus quinquestriatus hebraeus). It is a neurotoxin that blocks calcium-activated potassium channels.This blockade causes hyperexcitability of the nervous system. It is a close homologue of agitoxin and both toxins come from Leiurus quinquestriatus hebraeus.

- **Category:** Protein, Natural Toxin, Animal Toxin
- **Route of exposure:** injection (sting/bite)
- **Sources and uses:** Charybdotoxin is a peptide toxin produced by the Yellow scorpion (Leiurus quinquestriatus hebraeus).
- **Mechanism of toxicity:** Charybdotoxin is a neurotoxin that blocks calcium-activated and voltage gated potassium channels by binding to one of four independent, overlapping binding sites. This results in neuronal hyperexcitability.
- **Health effects:** Stings from the Yellow scorpion are painful but not usually fatal. However, in some cases envenomation may cause anaphylaxis, a potentially life-threatening allergic reaction to the venom.
- **Acute toxicity:** LD50: 0.25 mg/kg (Subcutaneous, Mouse)
