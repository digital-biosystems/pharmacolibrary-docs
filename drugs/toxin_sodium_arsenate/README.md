# Sodium arsenate

- **generic name:** Sodium arsenate
- **T3DB:** [T3D0298](http://www.t3db.ca/toxins/T3D0298) · **PubChem:** [CID 23677060](https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/compound/23677060)

## About

Sodium dihydrogen arsenate is the inorganic compound with the formula NaH2AsO4. Related salts are also called sodium arsenate, including Na2HAsO4 (disodium hydrogen arsenate) and NaH2AsO4 (sodium dihydrogen arsenate). Sodium dihydrogen arsenate is a colorless solid that is highly toxic.

<p><small>Source: <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sodium_dihydrogen_arsenate?oldid=1300113283">Wikipedia</a> · <a href="https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/">CC BY-SA 4.0</a></small></p>

- **Category:** Industrial/Workplace Toxin, Pollutant, Airborne Pollutant, Synthetic Toxin
- **Route of exposure:** oral, inhalation, dermal
- **Sources and uses:** Sodium arsenate was formerly used in the treatment of chronic skin diseases, in parasitic diseases of the blood, and in some forms of anaemia.
- **Mechanism of toxicity:** Arsenic and its metabolites disrupt ATP production through several mechanisms. At the level of the citric acid cycle, arsenic inhibits pyruvate dehydrogenase and by competing with phosphate it uncouples oxidative phosphorylation, thus inhibiting energy-linked reduction of NAD+, mitochondrial respiration, and ATP synthesis. …
- **Health effects:** Arsenic poisoning can lead to death from multi-system organ failure, probably from necrotic cell death, not apoptosis. Arsenic is also a known carcinogen, esepcially in skin, liver, bladder and lung cancers.
- **Carcinogenicity:** IARC group 1, carcinogenic to humans.
- **Minimal risk levels (ATSDR):** Acute Oral: 0.005 mg/kg/day 
Chronic Oral: 0.0003 mg/kg/day 
Chronic Inhalation: 0.01 mg/m3
- **Acute toxicity:** LD50: 41 mg/kg (Oral, Rat)
LD50: 1-20 mg/kg (Oral, Mouse)
