# Melphalan

- **generic name:** Melphalan
- **T3DB:** [T3D4702](http://www.t3db.ca/toxins/T3D4702) · **DrugBank:** [DB01042](https://go.drugbank.com/drugs/DB01042) · **PubChem:** [CID 460612](https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/compound/460612)
- **groups:** approved, investigational

## About

An alkylating nitrogen mustard that is used as an antineoplastic in the form of the levo isomer - melphalan, the racemic mixture - merphalan, and the dextro isomer - medphalan; toxic to bone marrow, but little vesicant action; potential carcinogen.

- **Category:** Drug, Synthetic Toxin
- **Route of exposure:** incomplete, variable, 25-89% post oral dose
- **Sources and uses:** For the palliative treatment of multiple myeloma and for the palliation of non-resectable epithelial carcinoma of the ovary. Has also been used alone or as part of various chemotherapeutic regimens as an adjunct to surgery in the treatment of breast cancer, alone or in combination regimens for palliative treatment of locally recurrent or unresectable in-transit metastatic melanoma of the extremities, as well as for the treatment of amyloidosis with prednisone.
- **Mechanism of toxicity:** Alkylating agents work by three different mechanisms: 1) attachment of alkyl groups to DNA bases (primarily at the N-7 position of guanine and to a lesser extent, at the N-3 position of adenine), forming monoadducts and resulting in the DNA being fragmented by repair enzymes in their attempts to replace the alkylated bases, preventing DNA synthesis and RNA transcription from the affected DNA, 2) DNA damage via the formation of cross-links (bonds between atoms in the DNA) which prevents DNA from being separated for synthesis or transcription, and 3) the induction of mispairing of the nucleotides leading to mutations.
- **Health effects:** The principal toxic effect is bone marrow suppression.
- **Carcinogenicity:** IARC group 1, carcinogenic to humans.
- **Acute toxicity:** LD<sub>50</sub>=11.2 mg/kg (orally in rat)
