# Vanadyl acetylacetonate

- **generic name:** Vanadyl acetylacetonate
- **T3DB:** [T3D1642](http://www.t3db.ca/toxins/T3D1642) · **PubChem:** [CID 5486770](https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/compound/5486770)

## About

Vanadyl acetylacetonate is a coordination complex of vanadium. It is used in organic chemistry as a reagent in the epoxidation of allylic alcohols in combination with tert-butyl hydroperoxide (TBHP). It also exhibits insulin mimetic properties, in that in can stimulate the phosphorylation of protein kinase B (PKB/Akt) and glycogen synthase kinase-3 (GSK3), as well as inhibit tyrosine phosphatase (PTPase). …

- **Category:** Industrial/Workplace Toxin, Synthetic Toxin
- **Route of exposure:** oral, inhalation, dermal
- **Sources and uses:** Vanadyl acetylacetonate is used in organic chemistry as a reagent in the epoxidation of allylic alcohols in combination with tert-butyl hydroperoxide (TBHP). It also exhibits insulin mimetic properties, in that in can stimulate the phosphorylation of protein kinase B (PKB/Akt) and glycogen synthase kinase-3 (GSK3), as well as inhibit tyrosine phosphatase (PTPase). …
- **Mechanism of toxicity:** Vanadium damages alveolar macrophages by decreasing the macrophage membrane integrity, thus impairing the cells' phagocytotic ability and viability. The pentavalent form of vanadium, vanadate, is a potent inhibitor of the Ca+-ATPase and Na+,K+-ATPase of plasma membranes, which decreases intracellular ATP concentration. …
- **Health effects:** Breathing high levels of vanadium affects the lungs, throat, and eyes. Ingestion of vanadium may cause kidney and liver damage, birth defects, or death.
- **Minimal risk levels (ATSDR):** Acute Inhalation: 0.0002 mg/m3; Intermediate Oral: 0.003 mg/kg/day
