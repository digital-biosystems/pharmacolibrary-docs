# Plutonium-239

- **generic name:** Plutonium-239
- **T3DB:** [T3D0121](http://www.t3db.ca/toxins/T3D0121) · **PubChem:** [CID 61782](https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/compound/61782)

## About

Plutonium-239 (239 Pu or Pu-239) is an isotope of plutonium. Plutonium-239 is the primary fissile isotope used for the production of nuclear weapons, although uranium-235 is also used for that purpose. Plutonium-239 is also one of the three isotopes that have been demonstrated to be usable as fuel in thermal spectrum nuclear reactors, along with uranium-235 and uranium-233. Plutonium-239 has a half-life of 24,110 years.

<p><small>Source: <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plutonium-239?oldid=1383754153">Wikipedia</a> · <a href="https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/">CC BY-SA 4.0</a></small></p>

- **Category:** Industrial/Workplace Toxin, Pollutant, Airborne Pollutant, Natural Toxin
- **Route of exposure:** oral, inhalation
- **Sources and uses:** Plutonium-239 is a key fissile component in nuclear weapons, due to its ease of fission and availability.
- **Mechanism of toxicity:** The alpha radiation plutonium emits does not penetrate the skin but can irradiate internal organs when plutonium is inhaled or ingested. Particularly at risk are the skeleton, where it is likely to be absorbed by the bone surface, and the liver, where it collects and becomes concentrated. …
- **Health effects:** Plutonium's radioactivity can cause cancers of the bone, liver, and lungs if ingested or inhaled. Large amounts may also cause acute radiation poisoning.
- **Carcinogenicity:** IARC group 1, carcinogenic to humans.
