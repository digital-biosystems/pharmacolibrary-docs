# Sodium nitrite

- **generic name:** Sodium nitrite
- **T3DB:** [T3D3551](http://www.t3db.ca/toxins/T3D3551) · **PubChem:** [CID 23668193](https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/compound/23668193)
- **groups:** approved, investigational

## About

Sodium nitrite is a chemical compound of sodium and nitrite ions. It is used mainly as a color fixative and preservative in meats and fish. Sodium nitrite may also be used in organic synthesis, in dyeing and printing textile fabrics, in photography, as a laboratory reagent and a corrosion inhibitor, and in the manufacture of rubber chemicals. …

- **Category:** Household Toxin, Industrial/Workplace Toxin, Food Toxin, Synthetic Toxin
- **Route of exposure:** oral, inhalation
- **Sources and uses:** Sodium nitrite is used mainly as a color fixative and preservative in meats and fish. Sodium nitrite may also be used in organic synthesis, in dyeing and printing textile fabrics, in photography, as a laboratory reagent and a corrosion inhibitor, and in the manufacture of rubber chemicals. …
- **Mechanism of toxicity:** Nitrite causes the autocatalytic oxidation of oxyhemoglobin to hydrogen peroxide and methemoglobin. This elevation of methemoglobin levels is a condition known as methemoglobinemia, and is characterized by tissue hypoxia, as methemoglobin cannot bind oxygen.
- **Health effects:** Nitrite poisoning causes methemoglobinemia. Nitrites may cause pregnancy complications and developmental effects. …
- **Lethal dose:** 10 to 100 mg/kg for an adult human.
- **Carcinogenicity:** Ingested nitrate or nitrite under conditions that result in endogenous nitrosation is probably carcinogenic to humans (Group 2A).
- **Acute toxicity:** LD50: 158 mg/kg (Intraperitoneal, Mouse)
LD50: 65 mg/kg (Intravenous, Rat)
LD50: 85 mg/kg (Oral, Rat)
LC50: 5.5 mg/L over 4 hours (Inhalation, Rat)
- **Treatment:** Methemoglobinemia can be treated with supplemental oxygen and methylene blue 1% solution administered intravenously slowly over five minutes followed by IV flush with normal saline. …
- **Detected in:** blood, cerebrospinal fluid, saliva, urine
