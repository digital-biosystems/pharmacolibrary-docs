# Plutonium-240

- **generic name:** Plutonium-240
- **T3DB:** [T3D0140](http://www.t3db.ca/toxins/T3D0140) · **PubChem:** [CID 104728](https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/compound/104728)

## About

Plutonium-240 (240 Pu or Pu-240) is an isotope of plutonium formed when plutonium-239 captures a neutron without undergoing fission. The detection of its spontaneous fission led to its discovery in 1944 at Los Alamos and had important consequences for the Manhattan Project.

<p><small>Source: <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plutonium-240?oldid=1347332253">Wikipedia</a> · <a href="https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/">CC BY-SA 4.0</a></small></p>

- **Category:** Industrial/Workplace Toxin, Pollutant, Airborne Pollutant, Natural Toxin
- **Route of exposure:** oral, inhalation
- **Sources and uses:** Plutonium-240 has a high rate of spontaneous fission, raising the background neutron radiation of plutonium containing it. Plutonium is graded by proportion of Pu-240. …
- **Mechanism of toxicity:** The alpha radiation plutonium emits does not penetrate the skin but can irradiate internal organs when plutonium is inhaled or ingested. Particularly at risk are the skeleton, where it is likely to be absorbed by the bone surface, and the liver, where it collects and becomes concentrated. …
- **Health effects:** Plutonium's radioactivity can cause cancers of the bone, liver, and lungs if ingested or inhaled. Large amounts may also cause acute radiation poisoning.
- **Carcinogenicity:** IARC group 1, carcinogenic to humans.
