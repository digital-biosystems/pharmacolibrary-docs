# Bromoform

- **generic name:** Bromoform
- **T3DB:** [T3D1785](http://www.t3db.ca/toxins/T3D1785) · **DrugBank:** [DB03054](https://go.drugbank.com/drugs/DB03054) · **PubChem:** [CID 5558](https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/compound/5558)
- **groups:** experimental

## About

Bromoform is an organic compound with the chemical formula CHBr3. It is a colorless liquid at room temperature, with a high refractive index and a very high density. Its sweet odor is similar to that of chloroform. It is one of the four haloforms, the others being fluoroform, chloroform, and iodoform. It is a brominated organic solvent. Currently its main use is as a laboratory reagent. It is very slightly soluble in water (one part bromoform in 800 parts water) and is miscible with alcohol, benzene, chloroform, ether, petroleum ether, acetone and oils.

<p><small>Source: <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bromoform?oldid=1380081668">Wikipedia</a> · <a href="https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/">CC BY-SA 4.0</a></small></p>

- **Category:** Industrial/Workplace Toxin, Pollutant, Airborne Pollutant, Synthetic Toxin
- **Route of exposure:** oral, inhalation, dermal
- **Mechanism of toxicity:** Bromine is a powerful oxidizing agent and is able to release oxygen free radicals from the water in mucous membranes. These free radicals are also potent oxidizers and produce tissue damage. …
- **Health effects:** Bromine vapour causes irritation and direct damage to the mucous membranes. Elemental bromine also burns the skin. …
- **Carcinogenicity:** IARC group 3, not classifiable as to its carcinogenicity to humans.
- **Acute toxicity:** LD50: 414 mg/kg (Intraperitoneal, Rat); LD50: 1400 mg/kg (Oral, Mouse); LD50: 1820 mg/kg (Subcutaneous, Mouse)
