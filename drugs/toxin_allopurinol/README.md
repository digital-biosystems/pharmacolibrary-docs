# Allopurinol

- **generic name:** Allopurinol
- **T3DB:** [T3D3477](http://www.t3db.ca/toxins/T3D3477) · **DrugBank:** [DB00437](https://go.drugbank.com/drugs/DB00437) · **PubChem:** [CID 2094](https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/compound/2094)
- **groups:** approved, investigational

## About

Allopurinol is only found in individuals that have used or taken this drug. It is a xanthine oxidase inhibitor that decreases uric acid production. It also acts as an antimetabolite on some simpler organisms. [PubChem] Allopurinol and its active metabolite, oxypurinol, inhibits the enzyme xanthine oxidase, blocking the conversion of the oxypurines hypoxanthine and xanthine to uric acid. …

- **Category:** Drug, Synthetic Toxin
- **Route of exposure:** approximately 80-90% absorbed from the gastrointestinal tract.
- **Sources and uses:** For the treatment of hyperuricemia associated with primary or secondary gout. Also indicated for the treatment of primary or secondary uric acid nephropathy, with or without the symptoms of gout, as well as chemotherapy-induced hyperuricemia and recurrent renal calculi.
- **Mechanism of toxicity:** Allopurinol inhibits the enzyme xanthine oxidase, blocking the conversion of the oxypurines hypoxanthine and xanthine to uric acid. Elevated concentrations of oxypurine and oxypurine inhibition of xanthine oxidase through negative feedback results in a decrease in the concentrations of uric acid in the blood and urine. …
- **Acute toxicity:** LD<sub>50</sub>=214 mg/kg (in mice)
