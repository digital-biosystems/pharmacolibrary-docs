# Potassium dichromate

- **generic name:** Potassium dichromate
- **T3DB:** [T3D0706](http://www.t3db.ca/toxins/T3D0706) · **PubChem:** [CID 24502](https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/compound/24502)

## About

Potassium dichromate is a chemical compound of potassium and hexavalent chromium. It is a common inorganic chemical reagent and is also used in photography and wood treatment. It is found naturally as the mineral lopezite. …

- **Category:** Household Toxin, Industrial/Workplace Toxin, Pollutant, Airborne Pollutant, Synthetic Toxin
- **Route of exposure:** inhalation, oral, dermal
- **Sources and uses:** Potassium dichromate is a common inorganic chemical reagent and is also used in photography and wood treatment.
- **Mechanism of toxicity:** Hexavalent chromium's carcinogenic effects are caused by its metabolites, pentavalent and trivalent chromium. The DNA damage may be caused by hydroxyl radicals produced during reoxidation of pentavalent chromium by hydrogen peroxide molecules present in the cell. …
- **Health effects:** Hexavalent chromium is a known carcinogen. Chronic inhalation especially has been linked to lung cancer. …
- **Lethal dose:** 1 to 3 grams for an adult human (hexavalent chromium).
- **Carcinogenicity:** IARC group 1, carcinogenic to humans.
- **Minimal risk levels (ATSDR):** Intermediate Oral: 0.005 mg/kg/day 
Chronic Oral: 0.001 mg/kg/day
- **Acute toxicity:** LD50: 26 mg/kg (Oral Rat); LD50: 403 mg/kg (Dermal, Rabbit); LD50: 37 mg/kg (Intraperitoneal, Mouse)
- **Treatment:** There is no know antidote for chromium poisoning. …
- **Detected in:** blood, cerebrospinal fluid, saliva, urine
