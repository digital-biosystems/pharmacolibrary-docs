# Zinc selenide

- **generic name:** Zinc selenide
- **T3DB:** [T3D0742](http://www.t3db.ca/toxins/T3D0742) · **PubChem:** [CID 4298215](https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/compound/4298215)

## About

Zinc selenide is the inorganic compound with the formula ZnSe. It is a lemon-yellow solid although most samples have a duller color due to the effects of oxidation. It is an intrinsic semiconductor with a band gap of about 2.70 eV at 25 °C (77 °F), equivalent to a wavelength of 459 nm. ZnSe occurs as the rare mineral stilleite, named after Hans Stille.

<p><small>Source: <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zinc_selenide?oldid=1380078149">Wikipedia</a> · <a href="https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/">CC BY-SA 4.0</a></small></p>

- **Category:** Industrial/Workplace Toxin, Pollutant, Airborne Pollutant, Synthetic Toxin
- **Route of exposure:** inhalation, oral, dermal
- **Sources and uses:** Zinc selenide is a semiconductor and is used in light-emitting diodes, diode lasers, x-ray and gamma ray detectors, and as as infrared optical material.
- **Mechanism of toxicity:** Anaemia results from the excessive absorption of zinc suppressing copper and iron absorption, most likely through competitive binding of intestinal mucosal cells. Unbalanced levels of copper and zinc binding to Cu,Zn-superoxide dismutase has been linked to amyotrophic lateral sclerosis (ALS). …
- **Health effects:** Chronic exposure to zinc causes anemia, atazia, lethargy, and decreases the level of good cholesterol in the body. It is also believed to cause pancreatic and reproductive damage. …
- **Carcinogenicity:** IARC group 3, not classifiable as to its carcinogenicity to humans.
- **Minimal risk levels (ATSDR):** Intermediate Oral: 0.3 mg/kg/day (Zinc) 
Chronic Oral: 0.3 mg/kg/day (Zinc)
Chronic Oral: 0.005 mg/kg/day (Selenium)
