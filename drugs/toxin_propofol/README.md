# Propofol

- **generic name:** Propofol
- **T3DB:** [T3D2900](http://www.t3db.ca/toxins/T3D2900) · **DrugBank:** [DB00818](https://go.drugbank.com/drugs/DB00818) · **PubChem:** [CID 4943](https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/compound/4943)
- **groups:** approved, investigational, vet_approved

## About

Propofol is an intravenous anaesthetic agent used for induction and maintenance of general anaesthesia. IV administration of propfol is used to induce unconsciousness after which anaesthesia may be maintained using a combination of medications. Recovery from propofol-induced anaesthesia is generally rapid and associated with less frequent side effects (e.g. drowsiness, nausea, vomiting) than with thiopental, methohexital, and etomidate. …

- **Category:** Drug, Synthetic Toxin
- **Route of exposure:** rapid - time to onset of unconsciousness is 15-30 seconds, due to rapid distribution from plasma to the CNS. Distribution is so rapid that peak plasma concentrations cannot be readily measured. Duration of action is 5-10 minutes. Parenteral (intravenous injection), inhalation, dermal
- **Sources and uses:** Its widespread use as an anesthetic induction agent has largely replaced that of sodium pentothal. It is also commonly used in veterinary medicine.. Used for induction and/or maintenance of anaesthesia and for management of refractory status epilepticus.
- **Mechanism of toxicity:** The action of propofol involves a positive modulation of the inhibitory function of the neurotransmitter gama-aminobutyric acid (GABA) through GABA-A receptors.
- **Health effects:** Mild myoclonic movements are common, as with other intravenous hypnotic agents. Another recently described rare, but serious, side effect is propofol infusion syndrome. …
- **Acute toxicity:** IV LD<sub>50</sub>=53 mg/kg (mice), 42 mg/kg (rats). Oral LD<sub>50</sub> (as a solution in soybean oil)=1230 mg/kg (mice), 600 mg/kg (rats)
