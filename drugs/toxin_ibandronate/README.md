# Ibandronate

- **generic name:** Ibandronate
- **T3DB:** [T3D2667](http://www.t3db.ca/toxins/T3D2667) · **DrugBank:** [DB00710](https://go.drugbank.com/drugs/DB00710) · **PubChem:** [CID 60852](https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/compound/60852)
- **groups:** approved, investigational

## About

Ibandronate is a nitrogen-containing bisphosphonate in the same class as alendronate and risedronate. Ibandronate inhibits osteoclast-mediated bone resorption. All of the bisphosphonates prevent the breakdown of bone by bone cells called osteoclasts. …

- **Category:** Drug, Synthetic Toxin
- **Route of exposure:** poorly absorbed (mean bioavailability following a 2.5 mg oral dose is about 0.6% compared to intravenous dosing). Absorption is impaired by any kind of food or drink other than plain water.
- **Sources and uses:** For the treatment and prevention of osteoporosis in postmenopausal women.
- **Mechanism of toxicity:** The action of ibandronate on bone tissue is based partly on its affinity for hydroxyapatite, which is part of the mineral matrix of bone. Nitrogen-containing bisphosphonates (such as pamidronate, alendronate, risedronate, ibandronate and zoledronate) appear to act as analogues of isoprenoid diphosphate lipids, thereby inhibiting farnesyl pyrophosphate (FPP) synthase, an enzyme in the mevalonate pathway. …
- **Acute toxicity:** LD<sub>50</sub> = 811 mg/kg (rat, oral)
