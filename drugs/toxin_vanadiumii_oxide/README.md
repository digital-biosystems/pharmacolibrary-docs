# Vanadium(II) oxide

- **generic name:** Vanadium(II) oxide
- **T3DB:** [T3D1630](http://www.t3db.ca/toxins/T3D1630) · **PubChem:** [CID 24411](https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/compound/24411)

## About

Vanadium(II) oxide is an oxide of vanadium. Vanadium is a transition metal with the chemical symbol V and atomic number 23. The element usually combines with other elements such as oxygen, sodium, sulfur, or chloride, and occurs naturally in about 65 different minerals and in fossil fuel deposits. …

- **Category:** Industrial/Workplace Toxin, Synthetic Toxin
- **Route of exposure:** oral, inhalation, dermal
- **Mechanism of toxicity:** Vanadium damages alveolar macrophages by decreasing the macrophage membrane integrity, thus impairing the cells' phagocytotic ability and viability. The pentavalent form of vanadium, vanadate, is a potent inhibitor of the Ca+-ATPase and Na+,K+-ATPase of plasma membranes, which decreases intracellular ATP concentration. …
- **Health effects:** Breathing high levels of vanadium affects the lungs, throat, and eyes. Ingestion of vanadium may cause kidney and liver damage, birth defects, or death.
- **Minimal risk levels (ATSDR):** Acute Inhalation: 0.0002 mg/m3; Intermediate Oral: 0.003 mg/kg/day
- **Treatment:** EYES: irrigate opened eyes for several minutes under running water. …
- **Detected in:** blood, cerebrospinal fluid, saliva, urine
