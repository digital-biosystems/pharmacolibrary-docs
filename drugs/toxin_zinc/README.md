# Zinc

- **generic name:** Zinc
- **T3DB:** [T3D0074](http://www.t3db.ca/toxins/T3D0074) · **DrugBank:** [DB01593](https://go.drugbank.com/drugs/DB01593) · **PubChem:** [CID 23994](https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/compound/23994)
- **groups:** approved, investigational

## About

A metallic element of atomic number 30 and atomic weight 65.38. It is a necessary trace element in the diet, forming an essential part of many enzymes, and playing an important role in protein synthesis and in cell division. Zinc deficiency is associated with anemia, short stature, hypogonadism, impaired wound healing, and geophagia. …

- **Category:** Drug, Household Toxin, Industrial/Workplace Toxin, Pollutant, Food Toxin, Natural Toxin
- **Route of exposure:** oral, inhalation, dermal (113)
- **Sources and uses:** Zinc has many commercial uses as coatings to prevent rust, in dry cell batteries, and can be mixed with other metals to produce alloys such as brass and bronze. Zinc compounds are widely used in industry to make paint, rubber, dyes, wood preservatives, and ointments.
- **Mechanism of toxicity:** Excessive zinc intake alters copper and iron absorption, most likely through competitive binding in intestinal mucosal cells. Stomach acid dissolves metallic zinc, producing zinc chloride, which is a corrosive product damaging the stomach lining. …
- **Health effects:** Chronic exposure to zinc causes anemia, atazia, lethargy, and decreases the level of HDL (good) cholesterol in the body. It is also believed to cause pancreatic and reproductive damages. …
- **Minimal risk levels (ATSDR):** Intermediate Oral: 0.3 mg/kg/day
Chronic Oral: 0.3 mg/kg/day
- **Acute toxicity:** LD50: 630 mg/kg (Oral, Rat)
- **Treatment:** Zinc poisoning is treated symptomatically, often by administering fluids such as water or milk, or with gastric lavage.
