# Arsanilic acid

- **generic name:** Arsanilic acid
- **T3DB:** [T3D0300](http://www.t3db.ca/toxins/T3D0300) · **DrugBank:** [DB03006](https://go.drugbank.com/drugs/DB03006) · **PubChem:** [CID 7389](https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/compound/7389)
- **groups:** experimental, vet_approved

## About

Arsanilic acid is an organoarsenic compound derived from orthoarsenic acid. It was once used as a human drug, but is now only found in veterinary medicine, where its use is controversial.

- **Category:** Industrial/Workplace Toxin, Synthetic Toxin
- **Route of exposure:** oral, inhalation, dermal
- **Sources and uses:** Arsanilic acid is used to treate dysentery in swine.
- **Mechanism of toxicity:** Arsenic and its metabolites disrupt ATP production through several mechanisms. At the level of the citric acid cycle, arsenic inhibits pyruvate dehydrogenase and by competing with phosphate it uncouples oxidative phosphorylation, thus inhibiting energy-linked reduction of NAD+, mitochondrial respiration, and ATP synthesis. …
- **Health effects:** Arsenic poisoning can lead to death from multi-system organ failure, probably from necrotic cell death, not apoptosis. Arsenic is also a known carcinogen, especially in skin, liver, bladder and lung cancers.
- **Lethal dose:** 130 mg for an adult human.
- **Carcinogenicity:** IARC group 3, not classifiable as to its carcinogenicity to humans.
- **Minimal risk levels (ATSDR):** Acute Oral: 0.005 mg/kg/day
Chronic Oral: 0.0003 mg/kg/day
Chronic Inhalation: 0.01 mg/m3
- **Acute toxicity:** LD50: 248 mg/kg (Intraperitoneal, Mouse)
LD50: 100 mg/kg (Intravenous, Mouse)
- **Treatment:** Arsenic poisoning can be treated by chelation therapy, using chelating agents such as dimercaprol, EDTA or DMSA. …
