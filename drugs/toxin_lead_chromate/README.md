# Lead chromate

- **generic name:** Lead chromate
- **T3DB:** [T3D0325](http://www.t3db.ca/toxins/T3D0325) · **PubChem:** [CID 24460](https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/compound/24460)

## About

Lead(II) chromate is an inorganic compound with the chemical formula PbCrO4. It is a bright yellow salt that is very poorly soluble in water. It occurs also as the mineral crocoite. It is used as a pigment (chrome yellow).

<p><small>Source: <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lead%28II%29_chromate?oldid=1380120420">Wikipedia</a> · <a href="https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/">CC BY-SA 4.0</a></small></p>

- **Category:** Industrial/Workplace Toxin, Pollutant, Airborne Pollutant, Synthetic Toxin
- **Route of exposure:** oral, inhalation, dermal
- **Sources and uses:** Lead chromate is used in pigments, as well as in some pyrotechnic compositions.
- **Mechanism of toxicity:** Lead mimics other biologically important metals, such as zinc, calcium, and iron, competing as cofactors for many of their respective enzymatic reactions. For example, lead has been shown to competitively inhibit calcium's binding of calmodulin, interferring with neurotransmitter release. …
- **Health effects:** Lead is a neurotoxin and has been known to cause brain damage and reduced cognitive capacity, especially in children. Lead exposure can result in nephropathy, as well as blood disorders such as high blood pressure and anemia. …
- **Carcinogenicity:** IARC group 1, carcinogenic to humans.
- **Minimal risk levels (ATSDR):** Chronic Inhalation: 0.05 mg/m3 (Lead); Intermediate Oral: 0.005 mg/kg/day (Hexavalent chromium) 
Chronic Oral: 0.001 mg/kg/day (Hexavalent chromium)
- **Acute toxicity:** LD50: >12 g/kg (Oral, Mouse); LD50: 400 mg/kg (Intraperitoneal, Guinea pig)
