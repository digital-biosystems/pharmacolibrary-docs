# 1,3-Dinitrobenzene

- **generic name:** 1,3-Dinitrobenzene
- **T3DB:** [T3D0782](http://www.t3db.ca/toxins/T3D0782) · **PubChem:** [CID 291796](https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/compound/291796)
- **groups:** experimental

## About

1,3-Dinitrobenzene (1,3-DNB) is a synthetic substance that is used in explosives. Nitrite is a toxic compound known to cause methemoglobinemia.

- **Category:** Industrial/Workplace Toxin, Synthetic Toxin
- **Route of exposure:** oral, inhalation, dermal
- **Sources and uses:** 1,3-Dinitrobenzene is used in explosives. Waste discharges from army ammunitions plants or other chemical manufacturers are the primary sources for release into the air, water, and soil.
- **Mechanism of toxicity:** In the red blood cell, 1,3-DNB induces formation of methemoglobin, leading to cyanosis. Reduction of the nitrogroup(s) of 1,3-DNB produces reactive nitroaromatic radical anions which redox cycle to produce other reactive, toxic species such as superoxide anion. …
- **Health effects:** Chronic exposure to 1,3-dinitrobenzene can cause a reduction (or loss) in the number of red blood cells (anemia). It may also cause behavioral changes and male reproductive system damage.
- **Carcinogenicity:** No indication of carcinogenicity (not listed by IARC).
- **Minimal risk levels (ATSDR):** Acute Oral: 0.008 mg/kg/day
Intermediate Oral: 0.0005 mg/kg/day
- **Acute toxicity:** LD50: 59 mg/kg (Oral, Rat)
LD50: 1990 mg/kg (Dermal, Rabbit)
LD50: 10 mg/kg (Intravenous, Dog)
LD50: 28 mg/kg (Intraperitoneal, Rat)
- **Treatment:** Treatment is mainly symptomatic and may include gastric lavage and/or the administration of activated charcoal. …
