# Carbon disulfide

- **generic name:** Carbon disulfide
- **T3DB:** [T3D0176](http://www.t3db.ca/toxins/T3D0176) · **PubChem:** [CID 6348](https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/compound/6348)

## About

Carbon disulfide is found in kohlrabi. Obsolete fumigant against insects in stored grain and potatoes.

- **Category:** Industrial/Workplace Toxin, Pollutant, Airborne Pollutant, Food Toxin, Natural Toxin
- **Route of exposure:** inhalation, oral, dermal, eye contact
- **Sources and uses:** Several industries use carbon disulfide as a raw material to make such things as rayon, cellophane, and carbon tetrachloride. Exposure to carbon disulfide can results from breathing air, drinking water, or eating foods that contain it. …
- **Mechanism of toxicity:** Carbon disulfide is a potent nerve toxin and also affect liver enzymes, particularly those related to lipid metabolism. The increases in serum cholesterol that are sometimes seen following carbon disulfide exposure may be a result of increased hepatic cholesterol synthesis. …
- **Health effects:** Following inhalation, subtle and transient changes in pulmonary function can be manifested as reduced vital capacity and decreased partial pressure of arterial oxygen. Patients can developed normochromic and normocytic anemia, eosinopenia, and an increase in reticulocyte cell numbers after oral exposure . Carbon disulfide poisoning can result in central nervous system depression, coma, respiratory paralysis, and death. …
- **Minimal risk levels (ATSDR):** Chronic Inhalation: 0.3 ppm
 Acute Oral: 0.01 mg/kg/day
- **Acute toxicity:** LD50: 3020 mg/kg/day (Oral, Mouse)
- **Treatment:** Following oral exposure, administer charcoal as a slurry (240 mL water/30 g charcoal). …
- **Detected in:** feces
