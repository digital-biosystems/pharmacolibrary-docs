# Uranium-234

- **generic name:** Uranium-234
- **T3DB:** [T3D0111](http://www.t3db.ca/toxins/T3D0111) · **PubChem:** [CID 61704](https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/compound/61704)

## About

Uranium-234 (234 U or U-234) is an isotope of uranium. In natural uranium and in uranium ore, 234U occurs as an indirect decay product of uranium-238, but it makes up only 0.0055% (55 parts per million, or 1/18,000) of the raw uranium because its half-life of just 245,500 years is only about 1/18,000 as long as that of 238U. Thus the ratio of 234 U to 238 U in a natural sample is equivalent to the ratio of their half-lives. The primary path of production of 234U via nuclear decay is as follows: uranium-238 nuclei emit an alpha particle to become thorium-234. Next, with a short half-life, 234Th nuclei emit a beta particle to become protactinium-234 (234Pa or more usually the isomer 234mPa). Finally, 234Pa or 234mPa nuclei emit another beta particle to become 234U nuclei.

<p><small>Source: <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uranium-234?oldid=1357836739">Wikipedia</a> · <a href="https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/">CC BY-SA 4.0</a></small></p>

- **Category:** Industrial/Workplace Toxin, Pollutant, Airborne Pollutant, Natural Toxin
- **Route of exposure:** oral, inhalation, dermal, radiation
- **Sources and uses:** Uranium is also used as a colorant in uranium glass, producing orange-red to lemon yellow hues.
- **Mechanism of toxicity:** Uranium is combined with either bicarbonate or a plasma protein in the blood but once in the kidney, it is released and forms complexes with phosphate ligands and proteins in the tubular wall, causing damage. Uranium may also inhibit both sodium transport-dependent and independent ATP utilization and mitochondrial oxidative phosphorylation in the renal proximal tubule. …
- **Health effects:** Uranium primarily damages the kidney, but may also damage the lungs, central nervous system, and immune system. Uranium's radioactivity is believed to damage the DNA, resulting in carcinogenic effects and reproductive and developmental damage.
- **Carcinogenicity:** IARC group 1, carcinogenic to humans.
- **Minimal risk levels (ATSDR):** Intermediate Inhalation: 0.0004 mg/m3 (Soluble uranium salts)
Chronic Inhalation: 0.0003 mg/m3 (Soluble uranium salts)
Intermediate Oral: 0.002 mg/kg/day (Soluble uranium salts)
Intermediate Inhalation: 0.008 mg/m3 (Insoluble uranium compounds)
