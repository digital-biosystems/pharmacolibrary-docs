# 1,1,2-Trichloroethane

- **generic name:** 1,1,2-Trichloroethane
- **T3DB:** [T3D0163](http://www.t3db.ca/toxins/T3D0163) · **PubChem:** [CID 6574](https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/compound/6574)

## About

1,1,2-Trichloroethane, vinyl trichloride or 1,1,2-TCA, is an organochloride solvent with the molecular formula C2H3Cl3 and the structural formula CH2Cl−CHCl2. It is a colourless, sweet-smelling liquid that does not dissolve in water, but is soluble in most organic solvents. It is an isomer of 1,1,1-trichloroethane, and a byproduct of its manufacture.

<p><small>Source: <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1%2C1%2C2-Trichloroethane?oldid=1360745861">Wikipedia</a> · <a href="https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/">CC BY-SA 4.0</a></small></p>

- **Category:** Industrial/Workplace Toxin, Pollutant, Airborne Pollutant, Synthetic Toxin
- **Route of exposure:** inhalation, oral, dermal
- **Sources and uses:** 1,1,2-Trichloroethane is used as a solvent. Exposure to 1,1,2-trichloroethane would most likely be from breathing vapors of the chemical or from skin contact. …
- **Mechanism of toxicity:** Acyl chlorides and free radicals formed during the metabolism of 1,1,2-trichloroethane are reactive metabolites that can bind to proteins and nucleic acids (DNA, RNA), and are suspected of being cytotoxic, mutagenic, and carginogenic.
- **Health effects:** Inhalation of high levels of 1,1,2-trichloroethanein can affect the nervous system and cause sleepiness. 1,1,2-Trichloroethane may also affect the liver, kidney, and digestive tract, produce skin irritation, and affect the body's ability to fight infections.
- **Carcinogenicity:** IARC group 3, not classifiable as to its carcinogenicity to humans.
- **Minimal risk levels (ATSDR):** Acute Oral: 0.3 mg/kg/day (Mouse)
Intermediate Oral: 0.04 mg/kg/day (Mouse) 9L422)
- **Acute toxicity:** LD50: 837 mg/kg (Oral, Rat) 
LD50: 5.38 g/kg (Dermal, Rabbit)
