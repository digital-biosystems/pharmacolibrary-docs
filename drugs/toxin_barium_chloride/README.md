# Barium chloride

- **generic name:** Barium chloride
- **T3DB:** [T3D1101](http://www.t3db.ca/toxins/T3D1101) · **PubChem:** [CID 25204](https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/compound/25204)

## About

Barium chloride is a chemical compound of barium. It is used in the laboratory as a test for sulfate ion. In industry, barium chloride is mainly used in the purification of brine solution in caustic chlorine plants and also in the manufacture of heat treatment salts, case hardening of steel, in the manufacture of pigments, and in the manufacture of other barium salts. …

- **Category:** Household Toxin, Industrial/Workplace Toxin, Synthetic Toxin
- **Route of exposure:** oral, inhalation
- **Sources and uses:** Barium chloride is used in the laboratory as a test for sulfate ion. In industry, barium chloride is mainly used in the purification of brine solution in caustic chlorine plants and also in the manufacture of heat treatment salts, case hardening of steel, in the manufacture of pigments, and in the manufacture of other barium salts. …
- **Mechanism of toxicity:** Barium is a competitive potassium channel antagonist that blocks the passive efflux of intracellular potassium, resulting in a shift of potassium from extracellular to intracellular compartments. The intracellular translocation of potassium results in a decreased resting membrane potential, making the muscle fibers electrically unexcitable and causing paralysis. …
- **Health effects:** The health effects of the different barium compounds depend on how well the compound dissolves in water or the stomach contents. At low doses, barium acts as a muscle stimulant, while higher doses affect the nervous system, causing cardiac irregularities, tremors, weakness, anxiety, dyspnea, paralysisand possibly death. …
- **Lethal dose:** 1 to 15 grams for an adult human (barium salts).
- **Minimal risk levels (ATSDR):** Intermediate Oral: 0.2 mg/kg/day 
Chronic Oral: 0.2 mg/kg/day
- **Acute toxicity:** LD50: 220 mg/kg (Oral, Rat); LD50: 178 mg/kg (Subcutaneous, Rat); LD50: 20 mg/kg (Intravenous, Rat)
- **Treatment:** Intravenous infusion of potassium often relieves many of the symptoms of barium toxicity.
- **Detected in:** blood, cerebrospinal fluid, saliva, urine
