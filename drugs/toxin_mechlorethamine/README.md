# Mechlorethamine

- **generic name:** Mechlorethamine
- **T3DB:** [T3D4204](http://www.t3db.ca/toxins/T3D4204) · **DrugBank:** [DB00888](https://go.drugbank.com/drugs/DB00888) · **PubChem:** [CID 4033](https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/compound/4033)
- **groups:** approved, investigational

## About

Mechlorethamine is only found in individuals that have used or taken this drug. It is a vesicant and necrotizing irritant destructive to mucous membranes. It was formerly used as a war gas. …

- **Category:** Cigarette Toxin, Drug, Synthetic Toxin
- **Route of exposure:** partially absorbed following intracavitary administration, most likely due to rapid deactivation by body fluids. When it is topically administered, systemic exposure was undetectable.
- **Sources and uses:** For the palliative treatment of Hodgkin's disease (Stages III and IV), lymphosarcoma, chronic myelocytic or chronic lymphocytic leukemia, polycythemia vera, mycosis fungoides, and bronchogenic carcinoma. Also for the palliative treatment of metastatic carcinoma resulting in effusion.
- **Mechanism of toxicity:** Alkylating agents work by three different mechanisms: 1) attachment of alkyl groups to DNA bases, resulting in the DNA being fragmented by repair enzymes in their attempts to replace the alkylated bases, preventing DNA synthesis and RNA transcription from the affected DNA, 2) DNA damage via the formation of cross-links (bonds between atoms in the DNA) which prevents DNA from being separated for synthesis or transcription, and 3) the induction of mispairing of the nucleotides leading to mutations. Mechlorethamine is cell cycle phase-nonspecific.
- **Carcinogenicity:** Chlormethine is probably carcinogenic to humans (Group 2A). …
- **Acute toxicity:** The oral LD50 for a rat is 10 mg/kg.
