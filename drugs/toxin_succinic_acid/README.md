- **groups:** approved, investigational, nutraceutical

## About

Succinic acid is a dicarboxylic acid. The anion, succinate, is a component of the citric acid cycle capable of donating electrons to the electron transfer chain. Succinic acid is created as a byproduct of the fermentation of sugar. …

- **Category:** Drug, Food Toxin, Natural Toxin, Animal Toxin
- **Route of exposure:** eye contact, Inhalation, Ingestion.
- **Sources and uses:** Succinic acid is a precursor to some specialized polyesters. It is also a component of some alkyd resins. …
- **Mechanism of toxicity:** Succinate can inhibit the activities of α-KG–dependent oxygenases (KDMs) and the TET family of 5-methlycytosine (5mC) hydroxylases. Succinate also mediates allosteric inhibition of hypoxia inducible factor (HIF) prolyl hydroxylases (PHDs). …
- **Health effects:** At acute doses or exposures succinic acid is a skin irritant. Chronically high doses of succinate can lead to succinylation or succination of a variety of enzymes. …
- **Carcinogenicity:** Not listed by IARC. …
- **Acute toxicity:** Acute oral toxicity: 2260 mg/kg [Rat].
