# Molybdenum

- **generic name:** Molybdenum
- **T3DB:** [T3D3957](http://www.t3db.ca/toxins/T3D3957) · **PubChem:** [CID 185498](https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/compound/185498)
- **groups:** approved

## About

Molybdenum is a transition metal with the atomic symbol Mo, atomic number 42, and atomic weight 95.94. The pure metal is silvery white in color, fairly soft, and has one of the highest melting points of all pure elements. Physiologically, it exists as an ion in the body. …

- **Category:** Household Toxin, Industrial/Workplace Toxin, Food Toxin, Natural Toxin
- **Route of exposure:** ingestion, inhalation
- **Sources and uses:** Molybdenum is important in plant nutrition, and is found in certain enzymes, including xanthine oxidase. Molybdenum is used to this day in high-strength alloys and in high-temperature steels. …
- **Mechanism of toxicity:** High levels of molybdenum can interfere with the body's uptake of copper, producing copper deficiency. Molybdenum prevents plasma proteins from binding to copper, and it also increases the amount of copper that is excreted in urine. …
- **Detected in:** blood, cerebrospinal fluid, saliva, urine
