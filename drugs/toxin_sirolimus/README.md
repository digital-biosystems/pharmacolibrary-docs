# Sirolimus

- **generic name:** Sirolimus
- **T3DB:** [T3D2921](http://www.t3db.ca/toxins/T3D2921) · **DrugBank:** [DB00877](https://go.drugbank.com/drugs/DB00877) · **PubChem:** [CID 46835353](https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/compound/46835353)
- **groups:** approved, investigational

## About

A macrolide compound obtained from Streptomyces hygroscopicus that acts by selectively blocking the transcriptional activation of cytokines thereby inhibiting cytokine production. It is bioactive only when bound to immunophilins. Sirolimus is a potent immunosuppressant and possesses both antifungal and antineoplastic properties. [PubChem]

- **Category:** Drug, Synthetic Toxin
- **Route of exposure:** oral
- **Sources and uses:** Used to prevent rejection in organ transplantation; it is especially useful in kidney transplants. [Wikipedia]
- **Mechanism of toxicity:** Sirolimus inhibits T lymphocyte activation and proliferation that occurs in response to antigenic and cytokine (Interleukin IL-2, IL-4, and IL-15) stimulation by a mechanism that is distinct from that of other immunosuppressants. Sirolimus also inhibits antibody production. …
- **Health effects:** Increased susceptibility to infection, lymphoma, and malignancy; bronchial anastomotic dehiscence in lung transplant patients; decline in renal function in long-term combination of cyclosporine with Rapamune; proteinuria.
- **Acute toxicity:** LD50: >800 mg/kg (oral, rat)
LD50: >800 mg/kg (oral, mice)
