## About

An aminoperhydroquinazoline poison found mainly in the liver and ovaries of fishes in the order tetraodontiformes, which are eaten. Tetrodotoxin is a potent neurotoxin that is produced by bacteria (genus Pseudoalteromonas, Pseudomonas, Vibrio) and found in certain species of Tetraodontiformes (puffer fish, porcupine fish, ocean sunfish, triggerfish), from which its name is derived. It acts on the voltage-gated sodium channels of nerve cells. …

- **Category:** Animal Toxin, Natural Toxin
- **Route of exposure:** oral, injection (sting/bite)
- **Sources and uses:** For the treatment of chronic and breakthrough pain in advanced cancer patients as well as for the treatment of opioid dependence. Tetrodotoxin is produced by bacteria (genus Pseudoalteromonas, Pseudomonas, Vibrio) and found in certain species of Tetraodontiformes (puffer fish, porcupine fish, ocean sunfish, triggerfish), from which its name is derived.
- **Mechanism of toxicity:** Tetrodotoxin binds to what is known as site 1 of the fast voltage-gated sodium channel. Site 1 is located at the extracellular pore opening of the ion channel. …
- **Health effects:** Tetrodoxin is a potent neurotoxin and produces paralysis, which may be followed by death due to respiratory failure.
- **Acute toxicity:** LD50: 334 ug/kg (Oral, Mouse)
