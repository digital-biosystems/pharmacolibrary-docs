## About

Ammonia is a colorless alkaline gas with a characteristic sharp smell. Ammonia is one of the most abundant nitrogen-containing compounds in the atmosphere. It is an irritant with a characteristic pungent odor, which is widely used in industry. …

- **Category:** Household Toxin, Industrial/Workplace Toxin, Food Toxin, Natural Toxin
- **Route of exposure:** oral, inhalation, dermal
- **Sources and uses:** Ammonia is used directly on farm crops, and is also a precursor to foodstuffs and fertilizers. It is also found in many household and industrial cleaners.
- **Mechanism of toxicity:** The topical damage caused by ammonia is probably due mainly to its alkaline properties. Its high water solubility allows it to dissolve in moisture on the mucous membranes, skin, and eyes, forming ammonium hydroxide. …
- **Health effects:** Acute exposure to high levels of ammonia in air may be irritating to skin, eyes, throat, and lungs and cause coughing and burns. Lung damage and death may occur after exposure to very high concentrations of ammonia. …
- **Lethal dose:** 2500 to 4500 ppm over 30 minutes for an adult human.
- **Minimal risk levels (ATSDR):** Acute Inhalation: 1.7 ppm
Chronic Inhalation: 0.1 ppm
- **Acute toxicity:** LD50: 350 mg/kg (Oral, Rat)
LC50: 3360 mg/m3 over 1 hour (Inhalation, Mouse)
Severe hyperammonemia is characterized by serum ammonia levels greater than 1000 μmol/L
- **Treatment:** Acute Exposure: EYES: irrigate opened eyes for several minutes under running water. …
- **Detected in:** blood, cellular cytoplasm, cerebrospinal fluid, urine
