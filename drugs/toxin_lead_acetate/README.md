# Lead acetate

- **generic name:** Lead acetate
- **T3DB:** [T3D0319](http://www.t3db.ca/toxins/T3D0319) · **PubChem:** [CID 9317](https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/compound/9317)

## About

Lead acetate is a chemical compound made by treating lead(II) oxide with acetic acid. Due to its sweet taste, it was used as a sweetner before its toxicity was recognized. Today it can be found in low concentrations in some hair dyes. …

- **Category:** Household Toxin, Industrial/Workplace Toxin, Pollutant, Airborne Pollutant, Synthetic Toxin
- **Route of exposure:** oral, inhalation, dermal
- **Sources and uses:** Lead acetate is used as a reagent to make other lead compounds and as a fixative for some dyes. It is also found in paints and varnishes, as well as in low concentrations in hair dyes.
- **Mechanism of toxicity:** Lead mimics other biologically important metals, such as zinc, calcium, and iron, competing as cofactors for many of their respective enzymatic reactions. For example, lead has been shown to competitively inhibit calcium's binding of calmodulin, interferring with neurotransmitter release. …
- **Health effects:** Lead is a neurotoxin and has been known to cause brain damage and reduced cognitive capacity, especially in children. Lead exposure can result in nephropathy, as well as blood disorders such as high blood pressure and anemia. …
- **Lethal dose:** 10 to 30 grams for and adult human (lead salts).
- **Carcinogenicity:** Organic lead compounds are not classifiable as to their carcinogenicity to humans (Group 3). …
- **Minimal risk levels (ATSDR):** Chronic Inhalation: 0.05 mg/m3
- **Acute toxicity:** LD50: 150 mg (Intraperitoneal, Rat); LD50: 104 mg/kg (Intravenous, Rat)
- **Treatment:** Lead poisoning is usually treated with chelation therapy using DMSA, EDTA, or dimercaprol.
- **Detected in:** blood, cerebrospinal fluid, saliva, urine
