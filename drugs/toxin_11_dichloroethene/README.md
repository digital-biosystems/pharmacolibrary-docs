# 1,1-Dichloroethene

- **generic name:** 1,1-Dichloroethene
- **T3DB:** [T3D0079](http://www.t3db.ca/toxins/T3D0079) · **PubChem:** [CID 6366](https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/compound/6366)

## About

1,1-Dichloroethylene, commonly called vinylidene chloride or 1,1-DCE, is an organochloride with the molecular formula CCl2CH2. It is a colorless liquid with a sharp odor. Like most chlorocarbons, it is poorly soluble in water but soluble in organic solvents. 1,1-DCE was the precursor to the original clingwrap, Saran, for food, but this application has been phased out.

<p><small>Source: <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1%2C1-Dichloroethylene?oldid=1381301137">Wikipedia</a> · <a href="https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/">CC BY-SA 4.0</a></small></p>

- **Category:** Industrial/Workplace Toxin, Pollutant, Airborne Pollutant, Synthetic Toxin
- **Route of exposure:** oral, inhalation, dermal
- **Sources and uses:** 1,1-Dichloroethene is used in the production of certain plastics as a comonomer in the polymerization of vinyl chloride, acrylonitrile, and acrylates. It is also used to make flame retardant coatings for fiber and carpet backings, and in piping, coating for steel pipes, and in adhesive applications.
- **Mechanism of toxicity:** 1,1-Dichloroethene toxicity is caused by its reactive metabolites, which include epoxides, acyl chlorides, and halogenated aldehydes generated via oxidation by cytochrome P-450 2E1. These metabolites, especially 2,2-dichloroacetaldehyde and 2-chloroacetyl chloride, damage the liver by binding to cellular macromolecules. …
- **Health effects:** 1,1-Dichloroethene mainly affects the central nervous system. It may lead to sedation, inebriation, convulsions, spasms, and unconsciousness. 1,1-Dichloroethene may also damage the livers, kidneys, and lungs.
- **Carcinogenicity:** IARC group 3, not classifiable as to its carcinogenicity to humans.
- **Minimal risk levels (ATSDR):** Intermediate Inhalation: 0.02 ppm
Chronic Oral: 0.009 mg/kg/day
- **Acute toxicity:** LD50: 200 mg/kg (Oral, Rat)
LC50: 6350 ppm/4 hr (Inhalation, Rat)
