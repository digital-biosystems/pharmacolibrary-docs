# Lead sulfide

- **generic name:** Lead sulfide
- **T3DB:** [T3D0336](http://www.t3db.ca/toxins/T3D0336) · **PubChem:** [CID 14819](https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/compound/14819)

## About

Lead sulfide is a sulfide of lead that occurs naturally as the mineral galena. It is used as a semiconductor and infrared detector. Lead is a heavy metal and stable element with the symbol Pb and the atomic number 82, existing in metallic, organic, and inorganic forms. …

- **Category:** Household Toxin, Industrial/Workplace Toxin, Pollutant, Airborne Pollutant, Natural Toxin
- **Route of exposure:** oral, inhalation, dermal
- **Sources and uses:** Lead sulfide is used as a semiconductor, infrared detector, and can be found in batteries and ceramic paints.
- **Mechanism of toxicity:** Lead mimics other biologically important metals, such as zinc, calcium, and iron, competing as cofactors for many of their respective enzymatic reactions. For example, lead has been shown to competitively inhibit calcium's binding of calmodulin, interferring with neurotransmitter release. …
- **Health effects:** Lead is a neurotoxin and has been known to cause brain damage and reduced cognitive capacity, especially in children. Lead exposure can result in nephropathy, as well as blood disorders such as high blood pressure and anemia. …
- **Lethal dose:** 10 to 30 grams for and adult human (lead salts).
- **Carcinogenicity:** IARC group 2A, probably carcinogenic to humans.
- **Minimal risk levels (ATSDR):** Chronic Inhalation: 0.05 mg/m3
- **Acute toxicity:** LD50: 1600 mg/kg (Intraperitoneal, Rat)
- **Treatment:** Lead poisoning is usually treated with chelation therapy using DMSA, EDTA, or dimercaprol.
- **Detected in:** blood, cerebrospinal fluid, saliva, urine
