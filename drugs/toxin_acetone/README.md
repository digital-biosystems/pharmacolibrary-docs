# Acetone

- **generic name:** Acetone
- **T3DB:** [T3D0184](http://www.t3db.ca/toxins/T3D0184) · **PubChem:** [CID 180](https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/compound/180)

## About

Acetone is one of the ketone bodies produced during ketoacidosis. Acetone is not regarded as a waste product of metabolism. However, its physiological role in biochemical machinery is not clear. …

- **Category:** Household Toxin, Industrial/Workplace Toxin, Food Toxin, Natural Toxin
- **Route of exposure:** inhalation, oral, dermal, eye contact
- **Sources and uses:** Most acetone produced is used to make other chemicals that make plastics, fibers, and drugs. Acetone is also used to dissolve other substances. …
- **Mechanism of toxicity:** Since acetone is highly water soluble, it is readily taken up by the blood and widely distributed to body tissues. Acetone may interfere with the composition of the membranes, altering their permeability to ions. …
- **Health effects:** Pulmonary congestion and edema can follow inhalation of acetone, which irritates the mucosa. Gastrointestinal hemorrhage caused by repeated vomiting of blood has been reported. …
- **Carcinogenicity:** No indication of carcinogenicity (not listed by IARC).
- **Minimal risk levels (ATSDR):** Acute Inhalation: 26 ppm
Intermediate Inhalation: 13 ppm 
Chronic Inhalation: 13 ppm
- **Acute toxicity:** LD50: 2400 mg/kg/day (Oral, Mouse)
- **Treatment:** Following oral exposure to acetone, consider insertion of a nasogastric tube to aspirate stomach contents only after recent, large acetone ingestions; symptomatic and supportive treatment is generally all that is required. …
- **Detected in:** blood, breast milk, cerebrospinal fluid, feces, saliva, urine
