# Cadmium chloride

- **generic name:** Cadmium chloride
- **T3DB:** [T3D0371](http://www.t3db.ca/toxins/T3D0371) · **PubChem:** [CID 24947](https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/compound/24947)

## About

Cadmium chloride is a white crystalline compound of cadmium and chloride, with the formula CdCl2. This salt is a hygroscopic solid that is highly soluble in water and slightly soluble in alcohol. The crystal structure of cadmium chloride (described below), is a reference for describing other crystal structures. Also known are CdCl2·H2O and the hemipentahydrate CdCl2·2.5H2O.

<p><small>Source: <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cadmium_chloride?oldid=1382996561">Wikipedia</a> · <a href="https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/">CC BY-SA 4.0</a></small></p>

- **Category:** Industrial/Workplace Toxin, Pollutant, Airborne Pollutant, Synthetic Toxin
- **Route of exposure:** oral, inhalation, dermal
- **Sources and uses:** Cadmium chloride is used in the dyeing and printing of fabrics, in photography, in producing electronics components and in the synthesis of other cadmium compounds.
- **Mechanism of toxicity:** Cadmium initially binds to metallothionein and is transported to the kidney. Toxic effects are observed once the concentration of cadmium exceeds that of available metallothionein, and it has also been shown that the cadmium-metallothionein complex may be damaging. …
- **Health effects:** Chronic exposure to cadmium fumes can cause chemical pneumonitis, pulmonary edema, and lung diseases such as bronchitis and emphysema. Cadmium also accumulates in the kidneys, causing permanent damage. …
- **Carcinogenicity:** IARC group 1, carcinogenic to humans.
- **Minimal risk levels (ATSDR):** Acute Inhalation: 0.00003 mg/m3 
Chronic Inhalation: 0.00001 mg/m3 
Intermediate Oral: 0.0005 mg/kg/day 
Chronic Oral: 0.0001 mg/kg/day
- **Acute toxicity:** LD50: 88 mg/kg (Oral, Rat); LD50: 1800 ug/kg (Intraperitoneal, Rat); LD50: 3200 ug/kg (Subcutaneous, Mouse)
