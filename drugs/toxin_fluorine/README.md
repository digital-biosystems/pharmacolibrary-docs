# Fluorine

- **generic name:** Fluorine
- **T3DB:** [T3D0211](http://www.t3db.ca/toxins/T3D0211) · **PubChem:** [CID 28179](https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/compound/28179)

## About

Fluorine (Latin: fluere, meaning to flow), is the chemical element with the symbol F and atomic number 9. It is a nonmetallic, diatomic gas that is a trace element and member of the halogen family. Pure fluorine is a corrosive, poisonous, pale yellowish brown gas that is a powerful oxidizing agent. …

- **Category:** Industrial/Workplace Toxin, Food Toxin, Natural Toxin
- **Route of exposure:** oral, inhalation, dermal
- **Sources and uses:** Fluoride compounds are used in making steel, chemicals, ceramics, lubricants, dyes, plastics, and pesticides. Fluorides are often added to drinking water supplies and to a variety of dental products, including toothpaste and mouth rinses, to prevent dental cavities.
- **Mechanism of toxicity:** Fluoride ions are incorporated into bone by substituting for hydroxyl groups in the carbonate-apatite structure to produce hydroxyfluorapatite, thus altering the mineral structure of the bone. Alteration in mineralization increases hardness and bone mass, but also decreases mechanical strength. …
- **Health effects:** Exposure to high levels of fluoride can result in denser bones. However, if exposure is high enough, these bones may be more fragile and brittle and there may be a greater risk of fracture. …
- **Lethal dose:** 25 ppm over 5 minutes for an adult human.
- **Carcinogenicity:** Inorganic fluorides used in drinking-water are not classifiable as to their carcinogenicity to humans (Group 3).
- **Minimal risk levels (ATSDR):** Acute Inhalation: 0.01 ppm
- **Treatment:** Oral exposure to fluoride compounds should be treated by giving milk, calcium carbonate, or milk of magnesia to slow absorption. …
- **Detected in:** blood, saliva, urine
